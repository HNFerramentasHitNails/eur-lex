4.1.3.    The Commission’s assessment
(18)      The results of the market investigation indicate that there is a plausible separate
          product market for container leasing that is separate from other types of container
          sourcing (i.e. sale of containers), without the need to segment the relevant product
          market any further. This is due to the following reasons.
(19)      First, with respect to the Parties’ argument that the sale and the leasing of
          containers are part of the same relevant product market, the Commission’s market
          investigation indicates that while the sale and the leasing of containers do
          constrain each other to some extent, they are not sufficiently substitutable to
          belong to the same product market.

          demand-side perspective, the choice of whether to lease or purchase containers is
          driven by different considerations. One competitor explained that customers that

          their expenses over the lease period’. (19) Another market respondent explained
          that leasing and buying are complementary rather than substitute strategies: ‘The
          choice between buying and leasing depends on the company’s financing capacity
          and the urgency of its needs. Leasing becomes the only option when an immediate
          solution is required, as purchasing containers takes several weeks.’ (20) Indeed,

(31)      In its past decisional practice, the Commission held that the relevant geographic
