5.      MARKET DEFINITION

5.1.    Description of fan coil units

(10)    FCUs cool and/or heat air by means of a coil, with the conditioned air being
        distributed to a room by one or more electrically driven fans. FCUs function in a
        similar way as AHUs. However, FCUs are smaller and lighter than AHUs,
        typically intended for controlling temperature in individual rooms or small areas.
        Traditionally, FCUs were mainly used in commercial buildings, often in
        combination with AHUs, while residential settings did typically not feature this
        combination. When FCUs were employed in a residential setting, it was primarily
        in southern European countries like Italy. However, they are increasingly employed
        for residential purposes across the EEA and in combination with other HVAC
        technologies such as heat pumps.

(11)    FCUs are available with different structures and in different designs. First, similar
        to other HVAC systems, FCUs may use different types of fans, such as, inter alia,
        centrifugal fans or axial fans. The type of fan is typically chosen on the basis of the
        desired air flow rate and pressure. FCUs are mainly built with centrifugal fans, as
        centrifugal fans have the ability to produce high pressure (but low volume) air flow
        that other types of fans cannot achieve.

6.1.      Legal framework

(37)      The Commission’s Guidelines on the assessment of horizontal mergers under the
          Council Regulation on the control of concentrations between undertakings51 (the
          ‘Horizontal Merger Guidelines’) distinguish two main ways in which mergers
          between actual or potential competitors on the same relevant market may
          significantly impede effective competition, namely non-coordinated effects and
          coordinated effects. The remainder of this Decision focuses on non-coordinated
          effects.

(38)      Non-coordinated effects may significantly impede effective competition by
          eliminating the competitive constraint imposed by one merging party on the other,
          as a result of which the Merged Entity would have increased market power without
          resorting to coordinated behaviour. According to recital 25 of the Merger
          Regulation, a significant impediment to effective competition can result from the
          anticompetitive effects of a concentration even if the Merged Entity would not
          have a dominant position on the market concerned. In this regard, the Horizontal
          Merger Guidelines consider not only the direct loss of competition between the
          merging firms, but also the reduction in competitive pressure on non-merging firms
          in the same market that could be brought about by the merger.52

(39)      The Horizontal Merger Guidelines list a number of factors, which may influence
          the extent to which horizontal non-coordinated effects arise from a merger, such as:
          the large market shares of the merging firms; the fact that the merging firms are
          close competitors; the limited possibilities for customers to switch suppliers; or the
          fact that the merger would eliminate an important competitive force. This list of
          factors applies if a merger would create or strengthen a dominant position or would
          otherwise significantly impede effective competition due to non-coordinated

6.2.3.1. Moderate combined market shares and small increments

(46)    As it can be seen in Table 1, the Parties combined market share on the only
        horizontally affected market remains moderate both in value and in volume, not
        exceeding 30% in the past three years. Furthermore, the increase resulting from the
        Transaction would not exceed 5% during the relevant period, except if sales in
        volume were to be considered in 2023. As it can be further seen in Table 1, the
        Merged Entity will continue to face strong competitors, such as Aermec, Daikin
        and Carrier.

(47)    The results of the market investigation do also not point to particularly large market
        shares of the Parties on the market for the supply of FCUs in general in Estonia,
        including on the narrower segment of non-classical FCUs.65

6.2.3.2. The Parties are not close competitors

(48)    The market investigation does not point to the Parties being close competitors in
        the supply of FCUs in general, or in the supply of non-classical FCUs in Estonia in
        particular. With respect to non-classical FCUs in Estonia, customers replied that
        Chiller, Daikin, Aermec and Mitsubishi would be closer competitors to Arbonia
        than Midea.66 Likewise, customers replied that Daikin and Mitsubishi would be
        closer competitors to Midea than Arbonia. 67 One customer noted specifically that
        Midea’s FCUs would have too high sound levels to be suitable for the Estonian
        market. 68

(49)    More generally, the majority of the market participants expressing their views
        considered that Midea and Arbonia do not compete more closely with each other
        than they do with other competitors. 69 Moreover, multiple market participants
        consider that Arbonia offers FCUs with higher quality or less noise than Midea.70

6.2.3.4. There is sufficient spare capacity on the market

(53)    During the market investigation, some market participants indicated that the Parties
        could be seen as important producers of white label FCUs. These white label FCUs
        are sold to original equipment manufacturers, the latter being able to sell these
        FCUs under their own brand.74 However, market participants have not raised any
        concerns with respect to FCUs production capacity;75 on the contrary, the results of
        the market investigation indicate that there is sufficient spare capacity on the
        market.76

(54)    Indeed, the majority of the competitors submitted that they do not operate at full
        capacity.77 One competitor even explained that ‘it is capable of doubling its
        production’.78 The perception of the production capacity on the market is further
        described by a customer who explained that ‘there is spare capacity on the
        European market for FCUs and that there is room to expand FCU production in
        the EEA.’79

(55)    Finally, the majority of the Parties’ customers and competitors who expressed their
        views during the market investigation believe that the Transaction will not have a
        negative impact on them.80

6.2.3.5. Conclusion

(56)    In view of the foregoing, the Commission concludes that the Transaction does not
        give raise to serious doubts as to its compatibility with the internal market or a
        substantial part of it, or with the functioning of the EEA agreement, in relation to
        the supply of non-classical FCUs in Estonia.
