(10)    The automotive driveline – or drivetrain – encompasses all the components
        responsible for transferring power from the engine (or electric motor) and
        transmission to the wheels of a vehicle. At its core, this system includes a
        combination of products such as driveshafts, differentials, joints, and axles. The
        interplay among these parts ensures that torque is effectively delivered to the drive
        wheels, whether the vehicle is accelerating in a straight line or manoeuvring
        through turns. (6)
(11)    Different driveline architectures use distinct configurations of these products. In
        vehicles powered by combustion engines, in a typical front-wheel drive (‘FWD’)
        layout with a front-mounted engine, the engine sends power to the front wheels
        through a transaxle. By contrast, rear-wheel drive (‘RWD’) typically locates the
        engine towards the front of the vehicle and uses a separate transmission that sends
        power through a propshaft to the rear differential and axle assembly. (7)
(12)    The driveline architecture also differs between all-wheel drive (‘AWD’) and four-
        wheel drive (‘4WD’), depending on whether they are designed for on-road
        performance (AWD) or for more demanding off-road applications (4WD). (8)
(13)    4WD vehicles have a longitudinal engine orientation. AWD vehicles may have a
        transverse or longitudinal engine layout, though most AWD vehicles have a
        transverse engine layout. (9)

5.      COMPETITIVE ASSESSMENT

5.1.    Overview of affected markets
(43)    The Transaction gives rise to affected markets only on the basis of a possibly
        relevant worldwide market definition, whereas no affected markets arise on the
        basis of the other plausible geographic dimension for these markets, i.e., EEA-
        wide (cf. Section 4.2.2, above). This is due to AAM’s overall limited presence in
        the EEA.
(44)    The Parties’ activities overlap horizontally in the possible worldwide markets for
        the manufacture and supply of each of (i) FDUs, (ii) PTUs, and (iii) RDMs (all
        excluding in-house production). In each of these possible worldwide markets,
        their combined market share would be at least 20% in 2024 (see Section 5.2.2.2,
        below), meaning that on the basis of a worldwide market definition these markets
        would be affected.
(45)    The Transaction also results in horizontal overlaps and vertical relationships in
        other possibly relevant markets. However, none of these give rise to affected
        markets within the meaning of recital 25(g) of Annex I to Regulation (EU)
        2023/914, (45) as they meet the conditions for review under point 5 of the Notice
        on Simplified Treatment. (46)
(46)    Namely, at the EEA-wide level, the Parties’ activities overlap (only) in the
        manufacture and supply of PTUs (and not at all in the manufacture and supply of
        FDUs or RDMs, which AAM does not sell in the EEA). However, the Parties’
        combined market share in this possible EEA market for PTUs is below 50% in
        2024 and the HHI increment (delta) resulting from the Transaction is below 150
        (due to AAM’s overall limited presence). Accordingly, this horizontal overlap
        does not give rise to a possibly relevant affected market, as it meets the conditions
        for review under point 5(d)(bb) of the Notice on Simplified Treatment.
(47)    Since there are no indications that the Transaction would lead to serious doubts
        on any of these other markets, these will not be further assessed in this
        decision. (47)
