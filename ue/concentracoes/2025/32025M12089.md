4.2.      Product markets

4.2.1.    PUP mattresses
(11)      PUP mattresses, sometimes referred to as “pressure relief” mattresses, are
          specifically designed to relieve pressure for patients with reduced mobility. They
          prevent the formation of pressure ulcers or sores, which are localised injuries to
          the skin or underlying soft tissue that occur as a result of long-term pressure on
          the body by re-distributing the pressure on the body. Pressure ulcers are most
          common in patients requiring long-term bedrest.
(12)      PUP mattresses differ from one another in the technology used to relieve pressure
          on the patient’s body. Foam PUP mattresses (sometimes also referred to as
          “static” foam mattresses) use high specification foam or gel. Air PUP mattresses
          (also referred to as “dynamic” mattresses) include alternating air cells to re-
          distribute pressure, often using an electric pump. Hybrid PUP mattresses combine
          both foam and air re-distribution technology in a single mattress.

4.2.1.1. The Commission’s past practice
(13)      The Commission has not previously assessed the market for PUP mattresses
          specifically, although in its decision in APAX/Kinetic Concepts, (6) it considered
          a neighbouring market for speciality hospital beds, related mattress replacements
          and mattress overlays and considered whether it would constitute a separate
          market from the market for conventional hospital beds. Ultimately, however, the
          Commission left the market definition open.

4.2.1.2. The Notifying Party’s views
(14)      The Notifying Party considers that all types of PUP mattresses are largely
          substitutable on both the demand and the supply-side. Therefore, the Notifying
          Party considers the relevant market should include all types of PUP mattresses.
(15)      On demand-side substitutability, even though they contain different materials and
          technologies, the Notifying Party submits that all types of PUP mattresses can
          generally be used interchangeably by a wide variety of patients. Foam/static,
          hybrid, and air/dynamic PUP mattresses are all appropriate to cater to patients at
          a “low” to “high” risk of developing ulcers, with hybrid and air/dynamic
          mattresses additionally able to address a “very high” risk patient group. Moreover,
          in the Notifying Party’s view, some hybrid and foam/static mattresses can achieve

(27)      The Commission considers that the market for healthcare beds can likely be
          segmented between those intended for long-term care (in non-acute settings,
          either in a care home or at-home setting) and those for hospital care (acute
          settings). A majority of respondents to the market investigation considered that
          long-term care beds and hospital beds are not interchangeable based on their
          functionalities and the needs of patients and caregivers, and that healthcare beds

          functionalities required in different settings, one customer noted that hospital beds
          must allow caregivers to carry out “medical functions” such as the ability to
          resuscitate patients, “which is not needed in a nursing home or at home.”

          import[ant] features” because they must be moved by at-home caregivers. (22)

          used in long-term care and those used in hospital care. The main differences are
          in aesthetic design, functionality, and cleaning requirements.” (23)

(28)      In light of the above, the Commission considers that, within healthcare beds, it is

          this decision, the exact definition of the relevant product market can be left open
