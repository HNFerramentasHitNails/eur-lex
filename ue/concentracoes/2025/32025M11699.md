          Netherlands. The Commission found that there are a number of similarities

4.2.2.2. The Notifying Party’s view
(31)      The Notifying Party does not consider the national markets of Belgium and the
          Netherlands, or the geographic market encompassing both countries, to represent
          the appropriate geographic scope for a long-life cream product, such as long-life
          dairy spray cream. The Notifying Party considers the geographic scope of the
          market to be EEA-wide, or at least consisting of the neighbouring countries of
          Belgium, the Netherlands, France and Germany, for the following reasons. (35)
          (a)    Due to its long shelf-life and non-perishable nature, long-life dairy spray
                 cream can be transported over longer distances and has similar
                 characteristics across different countries. Any remaining differences in
                 product specifications that still may exist between countries can be
                 addressed through flexible recipes from suppliers.
          (b)    Transport costs of long-life products are relatively low, allowing for an
                 efficient distribution throughout the EEA. The geographic scope may even
                 evolve towards global as part of the cans currently are already exported
                 worldwide.

4.3.      Supply of SMC

4.3.1.    Product market
(36)      SMC is a liquid concentrate produced through the removal of water from
          skimmed milk via, for example, evaporation. SMC is used, inter alia, in the
          production of yogurt, processed cheese, dairy drinks, dairy desserts, ice cream,
          and other reduced-fat dairy products. SMC is also used in confectionery and
          bakery products where a liquid milk component is required. (38)

4.3.1.1. Commission’s precedents
(37)      The Commission has not assessed to date a separate relevant product market for
          the supply of SMC. (39)

4.3.1.2. The Notifying Party’s view
(38)      The Notifying Party acknowledges the lack of precedents discussing product
          market definition specifically for SMC and argues that potential substitutes for
          SMC include skimmed milk, milk protein concentrates, and rehydrated skimmed
          milk powder (‘SMP’).

5.2.    Legal framework
(53)    The Commission’s guidelines on the assessment of horizontal mergers under the
        Merger Regulation (56) distinguish two main ways in which mergers between
        actual or potential competitors on the same relevant market may significantly
        impede effective competition, namely non-coordinated effects and coordinated
        effects. (57)
(54)    Non-coordinated effects may significantly impede effective competition by
        eliminating the competitive constraint imposed by one merging party on the other,
        as a result of which the merged entity would have increased market power without
        resorting to coordinated behaviour. According to recital 25 of the Merger
        Regulation, a significant impediment to effective competition can result from the
        anticompetitive effects of a concentration even if the merged entity would not
        have a dominant position on the market concerned. In this regard, the Horizontal
        Merger Guidelines consider not only the direct loss of competition between the
        merging firms, which is the most direct effect of the merger, but also the reduction
        in competitive pressure on non-merging firms in the same market that could be
        brought about by the merger. (58)
(55)    The Horizontal Merger Guidelines list a number of factors, which may influence
        the extent to which horizontal non-coordinated effects arise from a merger, such
        as the large market shares of the merging firms; the fact that the merging firms
        are close competitors; the limited possibilities for customers to switch suppliers;
        or the fact that the merger would eliminate an important competitive force. This
        list of factors applies if a merger would create or strengthen a dominant position
        or would otherwise significantly impede effective competition due to non-
        coordinated effects. Furthermore, not all of those factors need to present to make
        significant non-coordinated effects likely and the list itself is not an exhaustive
        list. (59)
