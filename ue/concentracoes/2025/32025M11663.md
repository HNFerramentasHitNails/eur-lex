4.     INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

(8)    The products relevant for the assessment of the Transaction in this decision
       (‘Decision’) are optical transport equipment. Optical transport equipment is used to
       transmit data over an optical fibre network. An optical fibre network is a
       communication system that uses light signals to transmit data through optical fibre
       cables over distances ranging from a few kilometres to across continents.

(9)    The predominant technology currently used to transmit data through optical fibre
       cables is called Wavelength Division Multiplexing (‘WDM’). Light has different
       wavelengths. WDM systems consolidate multiple wavelengths into a single fibre to
       increase the capacity of the network. Further, the introduction of the so-called
       coherent WDM technology has enabled greater volumes of data to be transmitted at
       higher speeds and over longer distances.5

4.1.    WDM system components and architectures

(10)    An optical fibre network typically consists of a physical optical fibre cable6 and
        optical transport equipment that enables the transmission of data through the cable.
        Optical transport equipment includes: (i) transponders, which are situated at each
        end of the cable to receive, convert, and send optical signals,7 as well as (ii) other
        components, which optimise the passage of the optical signal through the cable (so-
        called optical line system components). Optical line components include
        multiplexers (MUX), demultiplexers (DeMUX), optical amplifiers, and
        reconfigurable optical add/drop multiplexers (ROADM).8 Traditionally, all
        elements of these components are integrated (embedded) into a single chassis,9
        which is supplied by a single vendor as a full end-to-end solution.

(11)    However, recent technological developments have resulted in the possibility of
        disaggregating the optical fibre network through the use of new types of optical
        transport equipment, in particular ‘pluggable transceiver modules’. Similarly to
        transponders, pluggable transceiver modules can receive, convert, and send optical
        signals. However, unlike transponders that are integrated (embedded) into a chassis
        with other network components, pluggable transceiver modules are sold on a
        standalone basis and can be plugged into the ports of a chassis. Such disaggregated
        solutions allow customers to source different components from different vendors.
        Furthermore, pluggable transceiver modules tend to be smaller in size and consume
        less power compared to embedded transponders.10

(12)    Accordingly, WDM systems may either use an embedded transponder incorporated
        into the chassis in the form of a line card (so-called integrated or closed WDM
        systems), or a pluggable transceiver module plugged into the chassis’ ports (so-
        called disaggregated or open WDM systems).

(13)    Moreover, pluggable transceiver modules can be plugged directly into a switch or a
        router.11 That is called ‘coherent routing’. Coherent routing enables an alternative
        network architecture connecting these modules directly into switches/routers and
        thereby bypassing the need for transponders embedded in a chassis. According to
        the Parties, due to the elimination of the transponders and associated chassis (which
        represent a significant portion of system cost), coherent routing can lead to the
        simplification of the optical network and a reduction of the overall network costs.12

4.2.    Applications of WDM systems

(14)    Optical transport equipment is commonly distinguished, based on the distance
        covered, between (i) regional or metropolitan (‘metro’), or (ii) long-haul.
        Furthermore, based on the application served, optical transport equipment can be
        categorized as (iii) terrestrial, (iv) subsea, or (v) data centre interconnect (‘DCI’):

        •   Metro networks provide high-speed connectivity within a specific geographic
            area, typically a metropolitan area or a region. Metro networks were
            traditionally designed to transmit data over distances typically covering ranges
            up to 1 000 kilometres.13

        •   Long-haul networks provide high-bandwidth connectivity over countries or
            even continents typically covering ranges from 1 000 kilometres.14

        •   Terrestrial networks enable the transmission of data through fibre optic cables
            laid across land, connecting countries, cities, towns, and even rural areas.

        •   Subsea networks enable submarine cable data transmission in long-haul
            networks.15

        •   DCI networks enable high-speed connectivity between two or more data
            centres. DCI networks may directly connect two data centres (point-to-point),
            connect several data centres in a closed loop (ring), or connect them with
            multiple links (mesh). One of the key features of DCI networks is their capacity
            and speed to meet high data traffic demands.16

5.2.3.    The Commission’s assessment

(26)      In the market investigation, the Commission assessed whether it would be
          appropriate to identify a single market for the supply of optical transport

          management devices, aggregation devices and WDM systems), and, if so, whether

5.2.3.1. Potential segmentation by type of device (bandwidth management devices,
         aggregation devices and WDM systems)

(27)      The results of the market investigation are inconclusive as to whether the relevant
          market for the supply of optical transport equipment must be further segmented by
          type of device (into bandwidth management devices, aggregation devices and
          WDM systems). The feedback received from market participants during the market
          investigation confirmed that, despite the ongoing technological advancements in
          the market for the supply of optical transport equipment, the market transformation
          is still in progress. Thus, although there is a technological shift that has led to
          increased use of WDM systems, bandwidth management devices and aggregation
          devices are still relevant for the assessment.

(28)      First, the responses to the market investigation confirmed that the Commission’s
          past decisional practice is still relevant, as the vast majority of both competitors
          and customers having expressed a view submitted that the segmentation of the
          market for the supply of optical transport equipment between (i) bandwidth
          management devices, (ii) aggregation devices, and (iii) WDM systems, still reflects
          market reality.33

(29)      Second, the responses to the market investigation also confirmed that, whereas this
          segmentation (between bandwidth management devices, aggregation devices and
          WDM systems) is still relevant today, the use of the first two types of devices, i.e.,
          aggregation devices and bandwidth management devices (jointly referred to as
          ‘non-WDM devices’) is declining.

(30)      In this respect, the vast majority of competitors having expressed a view and the
          majority of customers having expressed a view confirmed that non-WDM devices
          are generally viewed as legacy products, and have been replaced to a large extent
          by WDM devices.34 Notably, even some competitors and customers who replied
          that the segmentation by type of device remains valid still today, acknowledged
          that the use and relevance of non-WDM devices is declining.

5.2.3.3. Potential inclusion of pluggable transceiver modules into the market for optical
         transport equipment

(41)    The market investigation yielded mixed feedback on whether pluggable transceiver
        modules (described in Section 4.1 of this Decision) should be included in the
        market for the supply of optical transport equipment. The responses to the market
        investigation confirmed that, whereas the market is undergoing a technological
        evolution, closed/integrated (excluding pluggable transceiver modules) and
        open/disaggregated WDM systems (including pluggable transceiver modules) are
        not fully substitutable at present.

(42)    From a demand-side perspective, it cannot be concluded that integrated and
        disaggregated WDM systems fully compete within the same product market and
        that the relevant market should therefore include pluggable transceiver modules.

(43)    Specifically, most customers having expressed a view responded that integrated
        and disaggregated systems currently are only partially substitutable based on
        product characteristics, prices and intended use.53 For instance, one customer
        submitted that ‘[t]he two models are not yet fully interchangeable because
        differences in performance optimization and operational complexity…’.54
        Similarly, the majority of competitors having expressed a view also submitted that
        integrated and disaggregated WDM systems are only partially substitutable.55 One
        competitor explained that ‘[w]hile there may be some overlap in functionality, they
        are not typically considered fully substitutable or interchangeable based on
        product characteristics, prices, and intended use. While there is a trend towards
        open and disaggregated systems in the industry, the transition from integrated to
        disaggregated systems is not always straightforward. It depends on factors such as

5.3.      Geographic market definition

5.3.1.    Commission’s precedents

(51)      In M.4003 Ericsson / Marconi and M.4214 Alcatel / Lucent Technologies,64 the
          Commission considered that the geographic market for optical transport equipment,
          and its possible segments, is at least EEA-wide, if not broader, for various reasons.
          These include customers and suppliers having an EEA (if not worldwide) presence,
          the evolution of IP technology and EEA/worldwide standardisation, the fact that
          prices and tenders for these products are normally negotiated on an EEA (if not
          worldwide) basis and that transport costs are not a determining factor when
          considering suppliers. Ultimately, the Commission left the exact geographic market
          definition open.

          there are no significant regulatory barriers for selling transceiver modules on a
          worldwide level; (iv) transportation costs are low and account for less than 1% of
