(25)    Pursuant to the Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines, (29) a vertical merger (i.e.

        to compete less effectively. (30)

5.2.2.    Customer foreclosure

(45)      Customer foreclosure may occur when a supplier integrates with an important
          customer in the downstream market. Because of this downstream presence, the
          combined entity may foreclose access to a sufficient customer base to its actual
          or potential rivals in the upstream market (the input market) and reduce their
          ability or incentive to compete. (38)

(46)      In assessing the likelihood of an anticompetitive customer foreclosure scenario,
          the Commission has to examine whether (i) the combined entity would have the
          ability to foreclose access to downstream markets by reducing its purchases from
          its upstream rivals, (ii) whether it would have the incentive to reduce its purchases
          upstream, and (iii) whether a foreclosure strategy would have a significant
          detrimental effect on consumers in the downstream market. (39)
(47)      Ability to foreclose. Carrefour Italia is unlikely to have the ability to pursue a
          customer foreclosure strategy against NewPrinces’ upstream competitors
          following the Transaction.

(48)      First, Carrefour Italia does not represent a significant customer for the upstream
          suppliers of fresh milk. Indeed, in the fresh milk markets (conservatively
          assuming these markets have a regional scope) Carrefour Italia’s purchasing share
          does not exceed [10-20]% in any region. Consequently, NewPrinces’ competitors
          would retain access to approximately [80-90]% of the customer base in each
          relevant market.

5.3.    Access to commercially sensitive information

(55)    Separate from foreclosure, as set out in paragraph 78 of the Non-Horizontal
        Merger Guidelines, non-horizontal non-coordinated effects can also arise when
        the merged entity, by vertically integrating, gains access to commercially
        sensitive information regarding the upstream or downstream activities of rivals.41
(56)    During the market investigation, some respondents expressed concerns that,
        following the Transaction, NewPrinces could gain access to commercially
        sensitive information that Carrefour Italia receives from NewPrinces’
        competitors, including price lists and details of promotional activities.
(57)    The Commission considers that NewPrinces will not have the ability to access
        any commercially sensitive information for the following reasons.
(58)    First, the information that upstream suppliers share with Carrefour Italia would
        not give NewPrinces meaningful insights into its competitors’ commercial
        strategies. Although suppliers’ price lists are typically identical for all customers,
        the final prices charged to each retailer are customer-specific and may deviate by
        as much as [70-80]% from the listed values. Accordingly, even if NewPrinces
        were to receive these price lists, they would not reliably indicate the prices that
        its competitors actually apply to other customers. With respect to promotional
        activities, upstream suppliers could choose to exclude Carrefour Italia from such
        initiatives, given its limited purchasing importance. Indeed, a major supplier to
        Carrefour Italia confirmed that it could restrict the information it shares with
        Carrefour Italia. (42)
