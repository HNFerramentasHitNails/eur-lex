5.      COMPETITIVE ASSESSMENT

5.1.    Analytical framework

(30)    The Commission Guidelines on the assessment of horizontal mergers under the
        Merger Regulation (the “Horizontal Merger Guidelines”) distinguish two main
        ways in which concentrations between actual or potential competitors on the same
        relevant market may significantly impede effective competition, namely
        non-coordinated effects and coordinated effects.36

(31)    A concentration giving rise to non-coordinated effects may significantly impede
        effective competition by eliminating the competitive constraint imposed by one
        merging party on the other, as a result of which the merged entity would have
        increased market power without resorting to coordinated behaviour. In that regard,
        the Horizontal Merger Guidelines consider not only the direct loss of competition
        between the merging firms, but also the reduction in competitive pressure on non-
        merging firms in the same market that could be brought about by the
        concentration.37 The Horizontal Merger Guidelines list a number of factors which
        may influence whether or not significant non-coordinated effects are likely to result
        from a merger, such as the large market shares of the merging firms, the fact that
        the merging firms are close competitors, the limited possibilities for customers to
        switch suppliers, or the fact that the merger would eliminate an important
        competitive force.38 Not all of these factors need to be present for significant non-
        coordinated effects to be likely. The list of factors, each of which is not necessarily
        decisive in its own right, is also not an exhaustive list.

(32)    A concentration may also significantly impede competition by changing the nature
        of competition in a market in such a way that firms that previously were not
        coordinating their behaviour, are now significantly more likely to coordinate and
        raise prices or otherwise harm effective competition.39 The Horizontal Merger
        Guidelines identify four cumulative conditions to establish the likely emergence

(44)    Fifth, the potential for coordinated effects appears to be limited by the lack of
        transparency of the market. Price negotiations between suppliers and customers
        reportedly take place on a bilateral basis at regular intervals, resulting in frequent
        and confidential price changes. In addition, as MCC is usually purchased alongside
        other bulk excipients, these negotiations tend to cover multiple excipients, making
        it very difficult for suppliers to monitor the net prices charged by their competitors
        and identify possible terms of coordination. While the results of the market
        investigation are not entirely conclusive, the majority of responding competitors
        indicate that the MCC market is not transparent. Some respondents who report that
        the MCC market is ‘relatively transparent’ refer to market intelligence efforts as
        well as the non-complex nature of the market as factors contributing to a certain
        degree of transparency. Other respondents point out that there is no cost model for
        this type of excipients, and that prices tend to vary significantly between suppliers
        and regions, suggesting that price transparency is limited.61 One customer for
        example reports that “[t]hough suppliers will be familiar with costing models and
        costing inputs, competitor pricing will not necessarily be too transparent as scale,
        region, manufacturing and costs to serve will vary from supplier to supplier, and
        region to region”.62 Overall, the market investigation confirms that price
        transparency in the MCC market is limited, making potential coordinated effects

(54)    Third, the market investigation confirms that the CCS market is and will remain
        competitive post-Transaction. Despite the Target Business’ leading position in the
        CCS market, a majority of the respondents to the market investigation indicated
        that there are and would remain a sufficient number of alternative suppliers post-
        Transaction able to supply the same CCS products and grades as the Parties.75 One
        respondent points out that “[t]here are several suppliers of CCS active in the EEA
        market offering a broad range of products at different price points”, so that
        customers will still have a variety of options to choose from post-Transaction.76

(55)    Fourth, as noted at paragraph (43) above, the Parties’ customers are large and
        sophisticated pharmaceutical companies with strong countervailing buyer power. A
        majority of the customers who responded to the market investigation reported
        having more than one qualified supplier of CCS.77 With regard to the possibility of
        switching suppliers or shifting orders between various suppliers, the considerations
        set out at paragraph (43) above in relation to MCC apply equally in the case of
        CCS.

(56)    Fifth, coordinated effects appear unlikely because of the lack of transparency of the
        CCS market. While the results of the market investigation are not entirely
        conclusive in this respect, the majority of responding competitors and customers
        consider that the CCS market is not transparent,78 for the same reasons as outlined
        at paragraph (44) above in relation to MCC. One competitor in particular reports
        that CCS pricing in the European and North American markets is not transparent.79
        Respondents who report that the CCS market is ‘relatively transparent’ generally
        refer to market intelligence (e.g. driven by distributors) as a factor contributing to
        some degree of transparency. However, most respondents consider that price
        transparency is limited, and a lot of customers report seeing significant price
        variations between suppliers in practice. Overall, the evidence from the market
        investigation confirms that price transparency in the CCS market is limited, making
        potential coordinated effects unlikely.
