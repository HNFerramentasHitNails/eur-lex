5.2.    Legal framework
(37)    According to the Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines, (41) the Commission’s
        assessment of vertical links focuses on the assessment of potential foreclosure
        concerns. Foreclosure occurs when actual or potential rivals' access to markets is
        hampered, thereby reducing those companies' ability and/or incentive to
        compete. (42) Such foreclosure can take two forms: (i) input foreclosure, when
        access of downstream rivals to supplies is hampered; (43) and (ii) customer
        foreclosure, when access of upstream rivals to a sufficient customer base is
        hampered. (44)
(38)    For input or customer foreclosure to be a concern three conditions need to be met
        post-Transaction: (i) the merged entity needs to have the ability to foreclose its
        rivals; (ii) the merged entity needs to have the incentive to foreclose its rivals; and
        (iii) the foreclosure strategy needs to have a significant detrimental effect on
        competition. (45)

5.4.2.    Customer foreclosure
(139)     The Commission considers that the Parties are unlikely to have the ability to
          engage in customer foreclosure by restricting access of other airports to SAS as
          provider of ground handling, cargo handling and line maintenance services. As
          indicated above, the geographic scope of the upstream market and that of the
          downstream markets for the provision of ground and cargo handling as well as
          line maintenance services is generally considered to be restricted to a specific
          airport. Ground handling and cargo handling activities are inherently local by
          nature, as they only support flights at particular airports. This means that these
          activities are not transferable between airports, which would negate CPH ability
          to engage into a customer foreclosure strategy.
(140)     In addition, even if it could be argued that any restriction of SAS’ demand for
          airport infrastructure services in other airports, such as Oslo or Stockholm
          Arlanda, would also impact SAS’ demand of associated commercial services at
          these airports (as a provider of ground/cargo handling or line maintenance
          services) resulting in customer foreclosure, (203) these customer foreclosure
          concerns have already been assessed and rejected as, inter alia, not merger specific
          in section 5.3.2 above.
