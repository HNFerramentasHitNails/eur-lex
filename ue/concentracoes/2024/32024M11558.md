4.3.1.3. The Commission’s assessment

(36)      The Commission’s market investigation did not indicate any reason to depart from

(37)      The majority of respondents that expressed a view consider that the plausible

          (i) cross-border and domestic contract logistics, (ii) the type of good handled or
          industry serviced and (iii) the type of provider into LLPs, 3PLs and 4PLs, remain
          relevant. For example, one market respondent stated that the market definition “is
          an accurate description of the current market structure from both a demand and
          offer perspective”.52

(38)      In light of the above, and for the purposes of this decision, the Commission
          considers that the exact scope of the product market definition for the supply of
          contract logistics services can be left open with respect to its plausible sub-
          segmentations as concerns the further segmentations into (i) into cross-border and
          domestic logistics; (ii) by the reference to the type of good handled or the industry
          serviced or; (iii) into LLPs, 3PLs and 4PLs, since the Transaction does not raise
          serious doubts as to its compatibility with the internal market under any plausible
          product market definition. The Commission will assess the effects of the
          Transaction by reference to the narrowest plausible product market delimitations,
          i.e., the markets for the provision of contract logistics services segmented based on
          the (i) type of operations (domestic or cross-border), (ii) type of good handled or
          industry serviced (i.e., consumer goods), and (iii) type of logistics provider (LLPs,
          3PLs and 4PLs).

          the merger, thereby reducing those companies’ ability and/or incentive to

(55)      The Non-Horizontal Guidelines distinguish between two forms of foreclosure.
          Input foreclosure occurs where the merger is likely to raise the costs of downstream
          competitors by restricting their access to an important input.78 Customer
          foreclosure occurs where the merger is likely to foreclose upstream competitors by
          restricting their access to a sufficient customer base.79

(56)      Foreclosure may also take more subtle forms, such as the degradation of the quality
          of input supplied. In its assessment, the Commission may consider a series of
          alternative or complementary possible strategies.80

(57)      In assessing the likelihood of an anticompetitive foreclosure scenario, the
          Commission examines, first, whether the merged entity would have, post-merger,
          the ability to substantially foreclose access to inputs or customers, second, whether
          it would have the incentive to do so, and third, whether such a foreclosure strategy
          would have a significant detrimental effect on competition.81

(58)      While the three above-mentioned factors (i.e. ability, incentive and effect) are often
          examined together as they are closely intertwined,82 the EU courts have confirmed
          that “those three conditions are cumulative, so that the absence of any of them is
          sufficient to rule out the likelihood of anti-competitive input foreclosure”.83

5.3.3.2. Incentive to foreclose

(81)     Since the Commission has concluded above in Section 5.3.3.1 that bpost would not
         have the ability to either restrict or degrade access to bpost’s domestic deferred
         B2C small package delivery services in Belgium, the question whether bpost would
         have the incentive to engage in such foreclosure strategies can be left open for the
         purposes of the present decision.

(82)     In any event, the Commission considers that, post-Transaction, the merged entity
         would not have the incentive to foreclose Staci’s competitors in the market for
         contract logistics (and its plausible segments) by restricting or degrading access to
         bpost’s domestic deferred B2C small package delivery services in Belgium.

(83)     First, in its assessment of the likely incentives of the merged entity, the
         Commission may take into account various considerations such as how the merged
         entity was behaving in the past.107 Pre-Transaction, bpost, which is active on the
         downstream market for contract logistics services, already offers its domestic
         deferred B2C small package delivery services in Belgium to downstream
         competitors in the market for contract logistics services and its plausible segments
         (paragraph (66)).

(84)     Second, the Notifying Party explained that other vertically integrated players such
         as DHL, UPS, FedEx, Colis Privé, DPD, Mondial Relay, GLS, PostNL and
         Amazon sometimes outsource a significant part of their volume to competing small
         package delivery providers.108 For example, bpost’s top five customers in the
         Belgian market for domestic deferred B2C small package delivery services include
         […].109 Amazon, a large retailer offering e-commerce contract logistics services,
         seems to be collaborating, apart from bpost, with Colis Privé, UPS and Chronopost
         in Belgium.110

(85)     Moreover, the Commission’s analysis of the Notifying Party’s internal documents
         has not yielded any results suggesting that the merged entity intends to either
         restrict or degrade access to its domestic deferred B2C small package delivery
         services for Staci’s competitors.
