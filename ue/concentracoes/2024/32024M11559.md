5.1.1.    Engineering services, procurement and construction

5.1.1.1. The Commission precedents

(16)      The Commission has not previously considered the market for the provision of
          EPC or GC services, in general, or in particular in relation to the semiconductors
          industry.

(17)      According to the Commission’s decisional practice, engineering services consist of
          providing intellectual services to support a project as a whole, from design to
          commissioning (engineering and processes, construction, operation and
          maintenance).6 The Commission has previously considered engineering services as
          a starting point for market definition and considered a potential further
          sub-segmentation of the provision of engineering services (i) by type or nature of
          activity/engineering service; and (ii) by type of industry sector (e.g. aeronautics,
          transport, energy, mining, etc.).7 However, the Commission ultimately left the
          precise product market definition open.

(18)      The Commission investigated whether the relevant product market concerns the
          provision of both EPC/GC and Engineering Procurement & Construction
          Management (‘EPCM’) services (where the contractor still manages all the
          subcontractor construction companies, but the contracts are entered into between
          the end customer and the subcontractors, so that the EPCM contractor does not
          have a direct legal relationship with the latter), or whether a further
          sub-segmentation, mainly based on the type of end customer, is appropriate.

5.1.1.2. The Notifying Party’s view

          planning, procurement, construction and installation of manufacturing sites in-

5.1.1.3.4.       Conclusion

(29)      The Commission considers that the results of the market investigation indicate that

          GC services for the semiconductors industry. This is mainly due to the technical

5.1.2.3. Conclusion

(40)      Based on the above, installation services for semiconductors are separate from
          installation services for other industries. Further, process piping services and tool
          installation services are distinct from other installation services (such as
          mechanical or electrical works). Therefore, for the purposes of the present case,
          installation services for semiconductors are to be understood as process piping
          services and tool installation services (which also include hook-up and
          MSS installation services).

(41)      For the purposes of assessing the Transaction, the Commission will therefore
          consider the market for installation services (process piping and tool installation) in
          the semiconductors industry. It can be left open whether a further distinction by
          type of installation service (i.e. process piping, tool installation, hook-up or MSS

5.2.1.    Engineering services, procurement and construction

5.2.1.1. The Notifying Party’s view

(70)      According to the Notifying Party, EXYTE and its competitors in the market for the

5.2.1.2. The Commission’s assessment

(71)      The Commission considers that the market for the provision of EPC/EPCM or
          GC services is narrower than global in scope.

(72)      First, when asked whether EPC/GC contractors from outside the EEA face certain
          challenges when participating in EEA tenders in order to supply their services for
          EEA-based projects, most market participants replied in the affirmative.84 This
          showcases that from a supply-side substitutability perspective, EPC contractors or
          GCs who are based in the EEA are advantaged compared to market operators
          offering their services outside of the EEA and are not necessarily in direct
          competition with them. As customers explained, this is due to the need for a
          EEA-based supplier network, knowledge of EEA tax regulations, labour law
          restrictions and easier customer communication.85 Similarly, presence in the EEA
          is appreciated due to relevant language skills86 and ease of after-sales support.87

(73)      Second, according to most of the Parties’ customers, who participated in the
          Commission’s market investigation, proximity of the EPC/GC services provider
          with the manufacturing site seems to be quite important for the success of the
          project. In this context, market participants note that ‘logistical proximity to the
          construction site (quick response times, etc.) is fundamentally necessary. Local
          market knowledge (Subcontractors etc.) is also very important’,88 given that
          ‘proximity to the project site is an enabler for [the] execution and success of the
          project’.89

5.2.1.3. Conclusion

(74)      In view of the above, the Commission considers that the market for the provision of
          EPC/GC services for the semiconductor industry is most likely EEA-wide in scope.

6.1.1.    Legal framework

(102)     The Commission’s Guidelines on the assessment of horizontal mergers under the
          Merger Regulation (the ‘Horizontal Merger Guidelines’) distinguish two main
          ways in which mergers between actual or potential competitors on the same
          relevant market may significantly impede effective competition, namely non-
          coordinated effects and coordinated effects.131

(103)     In particular non-coordinated effects may significantly impede effective
          competition by eliminating the competitive constraint imposed by one party to the
          concentration on the other, due to which the combined entity would have increased
          market power without resorting to coordinated behaviour. According to recital 25
          of the Merger Regulation, a significant impediment to effective competition can
          result from the anticompetitive effects of a concentration even if the combined
          entity would not have a dominant position on the market concerned. In this regard,
          the Horizontal Merger Guidelines consider not only the direct loss of competition
          between the merging firms, but also the reduction in competitive pressure on
          non-merging firms in the same market that could be brought about by the
          merger.132

(104)     The Horizontal Merger Guidelines list several factors, which may influence the rise
          of substantial non-coordinated effects from a merger, such as: the large market
          shares of the merging firms; the fact that the merging firms are close competitors;
          the limited possibilities for customers to switch suppliers; or the fact that the
          merger would eliminate an important competitive force. The list of factors also
          applies if a merger would create or strengthen a dominant position or would
          otherwise significantly impede effective competition due to non-coordinated
          effects. Furthermore, not all those factors need to be present to make significant
          non-coordinated effects likely and the list itself is not an exhaustive list.133

6.2.1.    Legal framework

(182)     Pursuant to the Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines,209 vertical concentrations may
          significantly impede effective competition if they give rise to foreclosure, which
          can be: (i) input foreclosure, where the concentration is likely to raise costs of
          downstream rivals by restricting their access to an important input upstream; or
          (ii) customer foreclosure, where the concentration is likely to foreclose upstream
          rivals by restricting their access to a sufficient customer base downstream.

         strategy is profitable. This depends on the trade-off between losses arising from
         restricting input sales and gains obtained upstream and downstream through
         diverted sales.213

(185)    In order to constitute a significant impediment to effective competition, the input
         foreclosure strategy has to be found to lead to increased prices in the downstream
         market.214 Further, the effect on competition needs to be assessed in light of
         efficiencies substantiated by the parties to the concentration.215

         customer in the downstream market. Because of this downstream presence, the
         combined entity may foreclose access to a sufficient customer base to its actual or
         potential rivals in the upstream market (the input market) and reduce their ability or
         incentive to compete.216

(187)    The incentive to foreclose depends on the degree to which it is profitable. The
         combined entity faces a trade-off between the possible costs associated with not
         procuring products from upstream rivals and the possible gains from doing so.217

         which the upstream division of the combined entity can benefit from possibly
         higher price levels in the upstream market arising as a result of upstream rivals

6.3.1.    Legal framework

(265)     According to the Non-Horizontal Guidelines, in the majority of circumstances,
          conglomerate mergers will not lead to any competition problems.277 However,
          foreclosure effects may arise when the combination of products in related markets
          may confer on the merged entity the ability and incentive to leverage a strong
          market position from one market to another closely related market by means of
          tying or bundling or other exclusionary practices. While tying and bundling have
          often no anticompetitive consequences, in certain circumstances such practices
          may lead to a reduction in actual or potential competitors’ ability or incentive to
          compete. This may reduce the competitive pressure on the merged entity allowing
          it to increase prices.278

(266)     In assessing the likelihood of anticompetitive foreclosure effects, the Commission
          examines, first, whether the merged firm would have the ability to foreclose its
          actual or potential competitors, second, whether it would have the economic
          incentive to do so and, third, whether a foreclosure strategy would have
          a significant detrimental effect on competition, thus causing harm to consumers.279

          bundling of the supply of MSS equipment and installation services, the Merged
          Entity is not expected to forego sales from the supply of MSS equipment, which
          can also be provided to fabs where the Merged Entity is not proximate close, in
          order to potentially increase its sales from the supply of installation services, which
          necessarily need some sort of geographic proximity between the fab and the
          respective supplier.318

6.3.4.2.3.      Impact on Competition

(315)     In relation to the possibility of the Merged Entity to engage in a conglomerate
          foreclosure tying conduct, it should be noted that end customers in this industry
          indeed tend to be highly sophisticated large international companies with
          experienced procurement and legal departments, who could arguably push back
          against a potential bundling attempt by the Merged Entity.

(316)     Further, it shall be reminded that there remain effective single-product/service
          market operators in both the market for the provision of installation services for the
          semiconductor industry in the EEA, as well as the one for the provision of MSS
          equipment, (and their potential sub-segments).319

(317)     As such, a bundling strategy by the Merged Entity would not, according to the
          Commission, have a meaningful impact on the available choice and terms of supply
          of installation services and MSS equipment (gas and chemical) for the
          semiconductor industry in the EEA.

6.3.5.    Conclusion on conglomerate effects

(318)     In view of the analysis in this section 6.3, the Transaction is unlikely to give rise to
          negative conglomerate effects and therefore does not give rise to serious doubts as
          to its compatibility with the internal market in this respect.
