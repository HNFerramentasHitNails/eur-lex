(22)    First, producers use the same assets and processes to produce related products. In
        the first place, in previous decisions, the Commission already found that, even if

        the reference production process is based on the same manufacturing sequence,

        jacketing - mechanical protection - external sheathing. The insulating and external
        jacketing manufacturing steps can be performed either with thermoplastic or
        thermosetting machineries (each suitable for a different insulating/jacketing
        material); the difference is driven by the specifications required by the final
        product based on its application. In other words, “General Wiring cables” include
        all cables with a common production process. The manufacturing sequence is

        specific for the final use/application (e.g. more simple cables like building wire or
        automotive cables might not require all the manufacturing steps).”35 In this regard,
        not only the manufacturing process is similar for different general wiring cables,

4.1.2.    Geographic market definition

4.1.2.1. Past Commission decisions

(31)      The Commission has previously considered that the geographic scope of the market
          for the manufacture and supply of general wiring is at least EEA-wide, taking into
          account specifically that cable specifications have been harmonised, that
          multinational actors on the market operate at a European level and that customers
          purchase general wiring from other EEA countries as well as from outside the
          EEA.52

4.1.2.2. The Notifying Party’s views

(32)      The Notifying Party agrees with the Commission’s prior decisions and considers
          that the market for general wiring is at least EEA-wide for the following reasons.

(33)      First, there are general wiring cable trade flows within the EEA, with high levels of
          imports and exports between regions. Most of the production capacity for general
          wiring in the EEA is concentrated in Germany, Italy and Poland, which produce
          more than their domestic needs. By way of example, Gruppo LTC, which produces
          exclusively in Italy, sells […]% of its general wiring production domestically. The
          Notifying Party further argues that, when focusing on four broad regions (Eastern,
          Northern, Western and Southern Europe), the majority of consumption for general
          wiring cables is imported from outside each region.53

(34)      Second, general wiring is easy to transport, and no special precautions are required.
          It is typically transported in regular sized trucks across Europe and manufacturers
          rely on third party logistics providers for delivery. Further, transport costs are low,
          accounting on average for […]% of the sales price and delivery times are short for
          cross-border sales. Transport costs and delivery ties are therefore no obstacle for
          customers to purchase products across the EEA.54

(35)      Third, the general wiring segment is not subject to significant regulatory barriers as
          the harmonization of the specifications between the EU Member States makes it
          easy for manufacturers to sell, and for customers to purchase the same products
          across the EEA. Even when national specifications exist, they require very
          marginal adaptation to the product designs and no specific investment (including
          R&D). For general wiring cables, type testing to obtain certifications last

5.3.      Horizontal effects

5.3.1.    Legal framework

(46)      The Horizontal Merger Guidelines69 describe two main ways in which horizontal
          mergers may significantly impede effective competition. In particular, the proposed
          concentration might be creating or strengthening a dominant position: (i) by
          eliminating important competitive constraints on one or more firms, which
          consequently would have increased market power, without resorting to coordinated
          behaviour (non-coordinated effects); or (ii) by changing the nature of competition
          in such a way that firms that previously were not coordinating their behaviour, are
          significantly more likely to coordinate and raise prices or otherwise harm effective
          competition (coordinated effects).

(47)      A merger giving rise to horizontal non-coordinated effects might significantly
          impede effective competition by creating or strengthening the dominant position of
          a single firm, one which, typically, would have an appreciably larger market share
          than the next competitor post-merger. Moreover, also mergers that do not lead to
          the creation or the strengthening of a single firm’s dominant position may create
          competition concerns under the substantive test set out in Article 2(2) and Article
          2(3) of the Merger Regulation. Regarding mergers in oligopolistic markets, the
          Merger Regulation clarifies that “under certain circumstances, concentrations
          involving the elimination of important competitive constraints that the merging
          parties exerted upon each other, as well as a reduction of competitive pressure on
          the remaining competitors, may, even in the absence of a likelihood of coordination
          between the members of the oligopoly, result in a significant impediment to
          effective competition”.70

          whether or not significant horizontal non-coordinated effects are likely to result
          from a merger, such as the large market shares of the merging firms, the fact that
          the merging firms are close competitors, the limited possibilities for customers to

5.3.2.1. The Notifying Party’s view

(49)      The Notifying Party considers that the Transaction will not give rise to competition
          concerns in the general wiring market for the following reasons.

(50)      First, the Notifying Party submits that their combined market share in the EEA in
          2023 was moderate ([10-20]%) with a low increment by Gruppo LTC ([5-10]%).72

(51)      Second, the Notifying Party submits that the market is fragmented and that several
          competitors are present. In 2023, Prysmian had a market share of [10-20]%, Tele-
          Fonika Kable a market share of [5-10]% and General Cavi a market share of [5-
          10]%.73

(52)      Third, the Notifying Party considers that the Parties are not close competitors,
          which is evidenced by the fact that, […].74 Further, companies active in the market
          for general wiring manufacture standardised products with low differentiation.
          According to the Notifying Party, general wiring is considered a commodity
          product produced in compliance with national or international standards in large
          volumes. The production process, comprising of drawing, cabling and extrusion,
          does not vary across manufacturers. No particular technology or know-how is
          required. As such, there is little room for differentiation between manufacturers in
          terms of technology or production method.75

          into the EEA.76 Further, standards are largely harmonized within the EU and

          facilitate the growth of manufacturers by providing services to end-customers and
          managing storage.78

          international companies that exercise buyer power, which will significantly restrain
          the combined entity’s behaviour and pricing.79 These customers are able to
          influence prices and price terms during negotiations and can refuse to buy other
          products from the Parties.80 Moreover, customers can credibly switch to
          alternatives suppliers and wholesalers can expand in-house production.81
