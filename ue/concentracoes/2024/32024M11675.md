7.      CONGLOMERATE EFFECTS

(31)    Conglomerate effects arise from combinations involving suppliers of products
        which belong to a range of products that is generally purchased by the same set of
        customers for the same end use. The main concern in the context of conglomerate
        mergers is that of foreclosure. The combination of products in related markets may
        confer on the merged entity the ability and incentive to leverage a strong market
        position from one market to another by means of tying or bundling or other
        exclusionary practices.26

(32)    Carlsberg and Britvic both supply a broad range of different beverages belonging
        to different product markets, which are typically purchased by the same set of
        customers (e.g. distributors, retailers or horeca customers) for the same end use
        (i.e. sale to final consumers for on-trade or off-trade consumption). However, the
        Commission considers that the merged entity will not be able to leverage its
        position to engage in exclusionary practices in these related markets.

(33)    First, in France, with the exception of Britvic’s supply of dilutables, the Parties’
        combined market shares do not exceed 30% on any of the markets concerned and
        are therefore unlikely to give rise to conglomerate concerns.27 With respect to
        Britvic’s supply of dilutables, a large majority of responding customers have
        indicated that Britvic’s products are only moderately important for them, or even
        not important at all.28 Moreover, as explained above, customers will continue to
        have access to well-established alternative suppliers of dilutables in France, such as
        Routin and Slaur. The merged entity will therefore be unable to leverage its supply
        of dilutables in France to foreclose competitors in related markets.

(34)    Second, in Ireland, Carlsberg is not directly active in the supply of beverages but
        acts through an independent beer bottler. This means that no tying or bundling of
        Carlsberg’s beer and Britvic’s non-alcoholic beverages will be possible in Ireland,
