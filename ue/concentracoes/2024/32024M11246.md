5.        MARKET DEFINITION

5.1.      Manufacturing and supply of luxury watches

5.1.1.    Product market definition

5.1.1.1. The Commission’s past practice

(12)      In previous decisions, the Commission has considered the manufacturing and
          supply of luxury watches as a separate relevant product market.10 The Commission
          further considered segmentations within luxury watches with a view to price bands
          (i.e., above/below EUR 5 000, 10 000, 50 000, 100 000) and luxury watches
          designed for men, women, and unisex. Ultimately, the Commission left the exact
          product market definition open.11

5.1.1.2. The Notifying Party’s view

(13)      The Notifying Party considers that manufacturing and supply of luxury watches
          constitutes a separate relevant product market. However, the Notifying Party
          argues that further segmentations of the market for manufacturing and supply of
          luxury watches are not warranted. In the Notifying Party’s view, there are no
          grounds for a segmentation with a view to price bands. The Parties do not
          recognise bright lines (i.e. specific thresholds, factors, or bands) in pricing of their
          luxury watches. Most manufacturers and suppliers in the EEA offer luxury watches
          across most of the price bands previously considered by the Commission. There are
          no insurmountable barriers to offering luxury watches across all these price
          bands.12

(14)      In the Notifying Party’s view, there are no grounds for a segmentation with a view
          to luxury watches designed for men, women, or unisex either. There are no longer
          clear objective criteria for ascertaining typical differentiating features for such
          luxury watches, such as a particular size of the watch or specific design (e.g.
          gemstones). There are no specificities in respect of manufacturing of luxury
          watches for respectively men, women, and / or unisex. The classification of
          a particular luxury watch as designed for men, women, or unisex used to be more
          of a reflection of each manufacturer’s respective promotional and marketing
          decisions.13

5.1.1.3. The Commission’s assessment

(15)      The results of the market investigation confirmed that a separate relevant product
          market for the manufacturing and supply of luxury watches exists.14

5.3.1.3. The Commission’s assessment

(41)      The results of the market investigation are not conclusive as to whether repair and
          maintenance services for luxury watches constitute a separate product market from
          the sale of such watches.46 A majority of retailers held that repair and maintenance
          services for luxury watches are not separate from the sale of luxury watches.
          Among other things, this is because of the high level of competence that is held by
          manufacturers and retailers of luxury watches and required to provide repair and
          maintenance services in this respect. Another reason is the end customer
          expectation to be able to receive repair and maintenance services from a retailer as
          part of a luxury watches’ lifecycle.47 However, watch manufacturers’ views in this
          regard were not conclusive48 with several referring to that it is an ancillary
          service.49

(42)      Nevertheless, the market investigation confirmed that there is not a single market
          for repair and maintenance services for all watch brands, as the majority of both
          retailers and watch manufacturers held that repair and maintenance services are
          specific to each watch brand,50 citing significant differences in terms of spare parts,
          tools, equipment and know-how needed.51

(43)      Ultimately, whether repair and maintenance services for Rolex and Tudor luxury
          watches are separate markets from the sale of these luxury watch brands can be left
          open, as the Transaction does not under any plausible definition give rise to serious
          doubts with regard to repair and maintenance services. For the purposes of this
          decision, the Commission will carry out its competitive assessment in relation to
          repair and maintenance services on the narrowest plausible market for Rolex
          and Tudor luxury watches separately.

6.4.    Vertical effects - Legal framework

(74)    According to the Commission’s Guidelines on the assessment of non-horizontal
        mergers under the Council Regulation on the control of concentrations between
        undertakings (‘Non-horizontal Merger Guidelines’), foreclosure effects may
        principally occur where actual or potential rivals' access to supplies or markets is
        hampered or eliminated as a result of the merger, thereby reducing these
        companies' ability and / or incentive to compete.78

(75)    In assessing the likelihood of an anticompetitive input foreclosure scenario, the
        Commission examines, first, whether the merged entity would have, post-merger,
        the ability to substantially foreclose access to inputs, second, whether it would have
        the incentive to do so, and third, whether a foreclosure strategy would have
        a significant detrimental effect on competition downstream.79 These three
        conditions are cumulative so that the absence of any of them is sufficient to rule out
        the likelihood of anti-competitive input foreclosure.

(76)    In assessing the likelihood of an anticompetitive customer foreclosure scenario, the
        Commission examines, first, whether the merged entity would have the ability to
        foreclose access to downstream markets by reducing its purchases from its
        upstream rivals, second, whether it would have the incentive to reduce its
        purchases upstream, and third, whether a foreclosure strategy would have
        a significant detrimental effect on end customers in the downstream market.80

(77)    The Commission also examines whether the merged entity may, by vertically
        integrating, gain access to commercially sensitive information regarding the
        upstream or downstream activities of rivals. For instance, by becoming the supplier
        of a downstream competitor, a company may obtain critical information, which
        allows it to price less aggressively in the downstream market to the detriment of
        end customers. It may also put competitors at a competitive disadvantage, thereby
        dissuading them to enter or expand in the market.81
