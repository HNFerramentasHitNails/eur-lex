(44)      First, the Parties’ market shares for MT retail distribution reflected in Table 1

          because the Parties’ retail market shares include direct sales by the represented
          OEMs via a retail outlet that in such case serves only as delivery point.61 In fact, a
          significant proportion of commercial vehicles, including MT, are being sold
          through framework agreements or tenders to larger professional customers. In
          many of these cases, the ultimate contract is concluded between, on the one hand,
          the OEM or the importer and, on the other hand, the professional end-customer.62

          dealer having any control over price or other contractual conditions.63 Where the
          contractual conditions, including price, are being negotiated between the OEM or
          importer and the end-customer directly, the retailer’s involvement has no impact on
          competition and, conversely, the direct sale has no impact on competition at the
          retail level and leaves the market position of the retailer unaffected.

5.3.2.    No competition concerns in vertically affected markets

(53)      The Transaction does not lead to competition concerns for the vertically affected
          markets for the following reasons.

(54)      First, the Transaction does not give rise to input foreclosure concerns. Iveco is not
          an important input for MT retail distribution in Sweden, as reflected by its
          moderate market share of only about [10-20]% at the wholesale level. Indeed, one
          respondent to the market investigation noted that the Transaction will have no
          significant impact because “Iveco is a small player in the Swedish market”.79
          Moreover, local brands such as Volvo Trucks and Scania have a strong and loyal
          customer base in Sweden.80

(55)      More broadly, MT do not appear to be a critical input for commercial vehicle
          retailers in Sweden.81 As shown in Table 1 above, the total number of annual MT
          sales in Sweden over the last three years were very small (i.e., only around 300-400
          per year). Considering the number of MT retailers and retail outlets across the
          country, MT sales are therefore unlikely to constitute an important source of
          revenue for them.

(56)      The Commission therefore considers that the Transaction cannot give rise to input
          foreclosure to the detriment of rival MT retailers who remain able to source from a
          multitude of wholesalers (including vertically integrated rival brands active on the
          wholesale level) and from a number of brands, many of which have a stronger
          position in Sweden than Iveco’s.

(58)    As a preliminary point, the Swedish Target Company currently sources all of its
        MT from its vertically integrated Iveco MT wholesale branch and does not sell at

        was not sourcing any MT from rival MT importers or wholesale distributors

        MT wholesalers. Therefore, the only type of customer foreclosure that could be
        envisaged is if Hedin Mobility ceased or reduced its purchases from rival MT
        wholesalers, OEMs or brands, which is unlikely, as set out below.

(59)    In the first place, Hedin Mobility has no ability to foreclose rival MT OEMs or MT

        Parties’ combined market share in the retail distribution market – i.e., the Parties’

        national ([30-40]%) and at a local level ([40-50]%),82 leaving a sufficient number
        of third-party retailers for rival wholesale distributors.

        Trucks (active on the retail level through its own brand, Volvo Truck) and
