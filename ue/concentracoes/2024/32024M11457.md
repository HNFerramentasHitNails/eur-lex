4.3.      WLAN equipment

4.3.1.    Product market definition

(35)      A wireless local area network (“WLAN”) is a LAN that is based on wireless
          connections. WLAN equipment includes wireless access points (“WAPs”) and
          WLAN controllers. A WAP is a networking device that allows wireless devices
          (e.g., computers, smartphones, etc.) to transmit and receive data over a WLAN. A
          WLAN controller is a hardware device that centralizes the control and management
          of WAPs to enable a large number of WAPs to be configured and managed at the
          same time.46

(36)      HPE supplies WAPs and WLAN controllers under its Aruba brand. Juniper
          supplies WAPs under its Mist brand.

4.3.1.1. Commission’s precedents

(37)      In Hewlett-Packard/3Com47 and Gores Group LLC/Siemens Enterprise
          Communications,48 the Commission considered, but ultimately left open, (i) a
          market for WLAN equipment that is separate from a market for LAN equipment,
          and (ii) distinct market segments for single components of the WLAN system (i.e.,
          WAPs and WLAN controllers).

4.3.1.2. The Notifying Party’s views

(38)      The Notifying Party considers that, from a demand-side perspective, WLAN and
          LAN equipment would not be effective and immediate substitutes to each other,

          creates the “wireless” area within the LAN). From a supply-side perspective, a
          large number of vendors provide both WLAN and LAN equipment, which
          according to the Notifying Party illustrates the complementarity of WLAN and
          LAN equipment.49

          provider network, provides internet connectivity.

(53)      Traditional routing relies on static configurations and limited transport options. SD-

          WAN.60

4.5.     Enterprise network security solutions

4.5.1.   Product market definition

(62)     Enterprise network security solutions are hardware or software products, or a

(203)    In assessing the likelihood of such a scenario, the Commission examines, first,
         whether the merged firm would have the ability to foreclose its competitors201,
         second, whether it would have the economic incentive to do so,202 and third,
         whether a foreclosure strategy would have a significant detrimental effect on
         competition, thus causing harm to consumers.203 These factors are cumulative and
         often examined together as they are closely intertwined.

(204)    In order to be able to foreclose competitors, the merged entity must have a
         significant degree of market power, which does not necessarily amount to
         dominance, in one of the markets concerned. The effects of tying or bundling can
         only be expected to be substantial when at least one of the merging parties’
         products is viewed by many customers as particularly important and there are few
         relevant alternatives for that product.204 Further, for foreclosure to be a potential
         concern, it must be the case that there is a large common pool of customers, which
         is more likely to be the case when the products are complementary.205 Finally,
         bundling is less likely to lead to foreclosure if rival firms are able to deploy
         effective and timely counter-strategies, such as single-product companies
         combining their offers.206

(205)    The incentive to foreclose competitors through tying or bundling depends on the
         degree to which this strategy is profitable.207 Bundling and tying may entail losses
         or foregone revenues for the merged entity.208 However, they may also allow the
         merged entity to increase profits by gaining market power in the tied goods market,
         protecting market power in the tying good market, or a combination of the two.209

(206)    It is only when a sufficiently large fraction of market output is affected by
         foreclosure resulting from the concentration that the concentration may
         significantly impede effective competition. If there remain effective single-product

6.3.3.    Foreclosure of the Juniper’s networking rivals by tying or bundling with HPE’s
          servers

(208)     Customers that purchase servers also purchase datacentre switches. HPE’s
          activities as a supplier of servers can therefore be considered as closely related to
          the supply of networking equipment, and in particular datacentre switches, in
          which Juniper is active.

(209)     As set out in Section 4.8, the market investigation was inconclusive as to whether a
          further segmentation of the market for the supply of servers is warranted, and, if so,
          on which basis. Given that HPE’s market share in 2022 exceeded 30% on the
          worldwide market for the supply of mid-range servers according to the
          segmentation applied by IDC,213 this section assesses whether the merged entity
          could foreclose its networking rivals by tying or bundling Juniper’s networking
          products, and in particular its datacentre switches, to HPE’s servers in this putative
          market.

6.3.3.1. The Notifying Party’s views

(210)     The Notifying Party submits that the merged entity would not have the ability to
          foreclose competing datacentre switches vendors by bundling HPE’s mid-range
          servers with its datacentre switches. In particular, the Notifying Party submits that,
          while some customers purchase mid-range servers and datacentre switches together
          from the same vendors, each solution is available and commonly sold on a
          standalone basis, and separate demand exists for each product.214

6.3.3.2. The Commission’s assessment

(211)     For the reasons set out below and based on the results of the market investigation,
          the Commission considers that the merged entity would not have the ability to
          foreclose Juniper’s networking rivals, in particular rival suppliers of datacentre
          switches, by tying or bundling the sale of Juniper’s datacentre switches to HPE’s
          mid-range servers. Therefore, given that the conditions under the Non-Horizontal
          Merger Guidelines are cumulative, the Commission does not need to take a
          position on whether the two other conditions are satisfied.215

(217)     In view of the above considerations and in light of the results of the market

          concludes that the Transaction does not raise serious doubts as to its compatibility

6.3.4.    Foreclosure of the Juniper’s networking rivals by tying or bundling with HPE’s
          HPC systems

(218)     Customers that purchase HPC systems purchase an integrated system of high-
          density servers, high-performance storage and high-performance networking
          fabrics. The latter are made up of various components, such as switches, adapters,
          cables and related software. Customers that purchase HPC systems therefore
          require switches.

(219)     Juniper does not supply HPC systems, nor high-performance networking fabrics
          such that there is no horizontal overlap between the Parties with regards to HPC
          systems. Juniper does supply datacentre switches. HPE’s activities as a supplier of
          HPC systems can therefore be considered as closely related to the supply of
          datacentre switches, in which Juniper is active.

6.3.4.1. The Notifying Party’s views

(220)     The Notifying Party submits that while Juniper supplies datacentre switches, its
          Ethernet-based datacentre switches are not optimised for HPC use cases. Unlike
          high-performance interconnects, they do not deliver the high-end performance in
          terms of bandwidth and latency. Therefore, the Notifying Party submits that these
          products cannot be considered to be complementary products.

(221)     To the extent that the Commission would consider that Juniper’s datacentre
          switches could be relevant for HPC use cases, the Notifying Party submits that this
          would only be relevant for low-performance HPC solutions, where customers
          would source servers, storage and interconnects separately rather than as part of an
          integrated HPC system. In such a scenario, the relevant related markets would be
          the markets for storage and servers, in which neither of the Parties have market
          power.

(222)     The Notifying Party further submits that the merged entity would in any event not
          have the ability to engage in a foreclosure strategy in respect of HPE’s HPC
          products. First, the Notifying Party argues that HPE does not have market power in
          either market, facing a wide range of competitors such as Dell, Lenovo, and others.
          Second, HPE already supplies both products and its market shares nevertheless
          remain limited, such that HPE could have already engaged in a hypothetical
          bundling strategy. Third, competing suppliers of datacentre switches supply a much
          broader range of customers than those who use it for HPC applications. Fourth, the
          Notifying Party submits that customers have significant countervailing buyer
          power and could switch to other providers of HPC systems in response to a
          bundling strategy.223

6.3.4.2. The Commission’s assessment

(223)    For the purpose of this Decision, the Commission considers that there is potentially
         a close relationship between HPE’s activities as a supplier of HPC systems and
         Juniper’s datacentre switches offering. While the majority of HPE’s HPC
         customers that expressed a view confirmed that only high-performance datacentre
         switches are suitable for HPC applications224, consistent with Commission
         precedent, the vast majority of respondents to the market investigation who
         expressed a view submitted that Juniper’s Ethernet-based datacentre switches are
         already or increasingly suitable for HPC applications. Juniper’s Ethernet-based
         datacentre switches are therefore potentially closely related to the HPC systems
         such as those supplied by HPE. The Commission will therefore assess this
         conglomerate relationship on the basis that the Transaction may have a significant
         impact on the worldwide market for Ethernet-based datacentre switches, given
         HPE’s market share of more than 30% in a potential market for HPC.

(224)    For the reasons set out below and based on the results of the market investigation,
         the Commission considers that the merged entity would not have the ability to
         foreclose Juniper’s networking rivals, in particular rival suppliers of datacentre
         switches, by tying or bundling the sale of Juniper’s datacentre switches to HPE’s
         HPC offering. Therefore, given that the conditions under the Non-Horizontal
         Merger Guidelines are cumulative, the Commission does not need to take a
         position on whether the two other conditions are satisfied.225

(225)    First, the merged entity does not have a significant degree of market power in a
         putative worldwide market for HPC systems. HPE had a market share between [30-
         40]% and [30-40]% over the past three years, facing competition from established
         players such as Dell, Lenovo, Atos and others, with Dell as the largest competitor
         at [20-30]% in 2023. Market shares have remained stable in the past few years, and
         HPE will continue to face competition post-Transaction.

(226)    Second, while many companies have a single department for the procurement of IT
         infrastructure226, the purchasing patterns for both products vary. The majority of
         HPE’s HPC customers that expressed a view in the market investigation noted that
         they do not tend to purchase HPC systems and datacentre switches at the same
         time.227

(227)    Third, the majority of respondents expressing a view in the market investigation

         advantage by offering Juniper’s datacentre switches as a bundle with HPE’s HPC

         technical requirements, even if there could be some cost efficiencies.228
         Furthermore, HPE today already supplies both HPC systems and datacentre
         switches, yet its market share on the market for the supply of datacentre switches
         remains limited ([0-5]% worldwide and [0-5]% in the EEA).229
