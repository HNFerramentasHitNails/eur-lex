5.2.1.3.3.       The Commission’s assessment

(30)      A clear majority of the respondents to the market investigation who expressed a view
          considered that the geographic scope of the markets for production and wholesale of
          fresh fruits and fresh vegetables are at least national, if not regional or EEA wide due to
          the more efficient transport solutions available nowadays.33

(31)      In any event, the exact product market definition can be left open as the Transaction

          any plausible market definition for the production and wholesale supply of fresh fruits
          and fresh vegetables. The Commission will assess the effect of the Transaction on the
          narrowest plausible geographic market, which is the national level, where the Parties
          have the highest market shares.

5.4.1.    Legal framework

(90)      The Commission’s guidelines on the assessment of horizontal mergers under the Merger
          Regulation (the ‘Horizontal Merger Guidelines’) distinguish two main ways in which
          mergers between actual or potential competitors on the same relevant market may
          significantly impede effective competition, namely non-coordinated effects and
          coordinated effects.80

(91)      Non-coordinated effects may significantly impede effective competition by eliminating
          the competitive constraint imposed by one merging party on the other, as a result of
          which the merged entity would have increased market power without resorting to
          coordinated behaviour. According to recital 25 of the Merger Regulation, a significant
          impediment to effective competition can result from the anticompetitive effects of
          a concentration even if the merged entity would not have a dominant position on the
          market concerned. In this regard, the Horizontal Merger Guidelines consider not only
          the direct loss of competition between the merging firms, but also the reduction in
          competitive pressure on non-merging firms in the same market that could be brought
          about by the merger.81

(92)      The Horizontal Merger Guidelines list a number of factors, which may influence the
          extent to which horizontal non-coordinated effects arise from a merger, such as: the
          large market shares of the merging firms; the fact that the merging firms are close
          competitors; the limited possibilities for customers to switch suppliers; or the fact that
          the merger would eliminate an important competitive force. This list of factors applies if
          a merger would create or strengthen a dominant position or would otherwise
          significantly impede effective competition due to non-coordinated effects. Furthermore,

6.       CONGLOMERATE EFFECTS

(183)    During the market investigation, the Interested Third Parties also referred to the
         existence of conglomerate links between the activities of PV Group (through its
         subsidiary PPD) for the supply of natural gas (in Croatia and Slovenia) and electricity
         (in Croatia) and the activities of Fortenova for the production of food products and the
         retail distribution of daily consumer goods (in Croatia and Slovenia).204

(184)    Conglomerate mergers are mergers between firms that are in a relationship which is
         neither purely horizontal (as competitors in the same relevant market) nor vertical (as
         supplier and customer). Conglomerate links arise when the parties to a concentration are
         active in closely related markets, for instance because they supply complementary
         products to the same set of customers or because they supply a range of products that is
         generally purchased by the same set of customers for the same end use.205

(185)    In light of this definition, PV Group and Fortenova do not operate on closely related
         markets with respect to the supply of natural gas and electricity, on the one hand, and
         the retail distribution of daily consumer goods, on the other hand.

(186)    With respect to electricity, PV Group exclusively supplies large industrial customers
         and does not supply the same customers as those to whom Fortenova distributes daily
         consumer goods (i.e. end consumers).206

(187)    As regards natural gas, PV Group is active in the retail supply of gas, however the
         customers of PV Group on this market do not purchase gas together with daily
         consumer goods and the specific characteristics of these products would make it
         impossible for the merged entity to tie or bundle these products. In any event, the
         market shares of PV Group on the retail market of gas remain limited in Croatia and
         Slovenia (respectively [20-30]%207 and <[0-5]%)208 like Fortenova’s market share for
         the retail distribution of daily consumer goods at national level in these two countries
