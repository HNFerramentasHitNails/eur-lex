5.        RELEVANT MARKETS

5.1.      Product Market Definition

(11)      The activities of the Parties overlap in relation to the provision of wet-leasing
          services. Wet-leasing involves the leasing of an aircraft to an airline with crew.
          Under a wet-leasing agreement, the lessor operates the flights using its own air
          operator certificate (AOC) and resources, for which it receives an income from the
          lessee, which is usually a fixed price per block hour, unrelated to ticket prices.

(12)      Damp-leasing is very similar to wet-leasing. The only difference is that with damp-
          lease services, the cabin crew is provided by the lessee, and not the lessor, whereas
          the lessor still provides the cockpit crew.

(13)      Where wet- or damp-lease services also include maintenance and insurance, one
          speaks of ACMI services (i.e. aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance services).
          ACMI services can be considered synonymous with wet- and damp-lease services.

5.1.1.    The Commission’s findings in the Clearance Decision

(14)      In the Clearance Decision, the Commission found that dry-leasing, wet-leasing and
          franchise services14 each belong to separate product markets.15 This finding is in
          line with the Commission’s past and more recent decisional practice.16

(15)      Furthermore, in the Clearance Decision, the Commission considered, but, in the
          absence of competition concerns, left open, whether damp- and wet-leasing of
          aircraft belong to the same product market.17

(16)      Finally, in the Clearance Decision, the Commission considered, but ultimately left
          open, whether the market for wet-leasing services should be further segmented to
          wet-leasing services of regional aircraft (i.e. aircraft with a capacity of 30-90+
          seats), and whether it should be further segmented by seat capacity of the regional
          aircraft between: (i) under 50 seats; and, above 50 seats; (ii) 50-70 seats; and,
          70-100 seats; and (iii) 30-70 seats; and, 70-100+ seats.18

5.1.4.    Conclusion

(23)      In light of the above, the Commission concludes that, for the purposes of this
          decision, wet-leasing, dry-leasing and franchise services constitute distinct product
          markets. Furthermore, it could be left open whether (i) wet and damp-lease services
          should belong to the same product market; and, (ii) the market for the provision of
          wet-lease services should be further segmented based on the size of aircraft, as the
          Transaction does not raise serious doubts as to its compatibility with the internal
          market or the functioning of the EEA Agreement, under any of the plausible
          product market definitions.

(24)      Nevertheless, since CityJet and Air Nostrum only provided wet-leasing services in
          the past four years, the Commission will only assess this market segment further in
          this decision. Moreover, considering that the Parties both use regional aircraft of a
          seat capacity between 88 and 100, the Commission will assess the effects of the
          Transaction on (i) the broader market for wet-leasing services; (ii) the segment for
          wet-leasing services of regional aircraft (with a capacity of 30-90+ seats); and, (iii)
          the sub-segment large regional aircraft (with a capacity of 70-90+ seats), in line
          with the findings of the Clearance Decision.

5.2.4.    Conclusion

(28)      In light of the above, the Commission concludes that, for the purposes of this
          decision, the question whether the scope of the market for the provision of wet-
          leasing services is EEA-wide or larger than EEA-wide can be left open, as the
          Transaction does not raise serious doubts as to its compatibility with the internal
          market or the functioning of the EEA Agreement, under any of the plausible
          geographic market definitions. Nevertheless, the Commission will assess the
          effects of the Transaction on the narrowest possible geographic market definition,
          namely the EEA.32

(50)    Whilst under Article 2(1)(b) of the Merger Regulation, economic and financial
        power are important criteria for the appraisal of whether a concentration is
        compatible with the common market, in the absence of other indicators, financial
        strength as such will not be sufficient to lead to the creation or strengthening of a
        dominant position as a result of which competition will be significantly impeded.
        In the present case, as outlined above in Section 6.1.2., market share overlaps are
        not indicative of creation or strengthening of a dominant position. Moreover, the
        market investigation provided no indications that by means of their financial
        power, the Parties will be able to increase the JV’s market position, for instance, by
        systematically undercutting competitors’ prices in such a way that its existing or
        potential competitors are marginalised.

(51)    Furthermore, the more significant aid granted to Air Nostrum by SEPI (AN aid
        2 and 3) was given to it while it was experiencing serious financial difficulties in
        the context of the Covid-19 pandemic. The aid is part of a series of measures
        conceived at national level by the Spanish authorities to remedy a serious
        disturbance in their economy. It aims at remedying the liquidity shortage and
        ensuring that the disruptions caused by COVID-19 outbreak do not undermine the
        company’s viability, and takes the form of a guarantee on a loan (AN aid 2) and a
        loan (AN aid 3). Both measures have been approved by the Commission and the
        use of the funds is subject to ongoing monitoring by Spain and by the Commission.

(52)    Consequently, the Commission sees no circumstances specific to the merger
        whereby such aid would alter its competition analysis under the Merger Regulation
        as set out above. In any event, this decision is entirely without prejudice to the
        Commission’s findings in carrying out state aid control.72

(53)    In light of all above considerations, the Commission concludes that the Transaction
        is unlikely to raise serious doubts as to its compatibility with the internal market in
        relation to wet-leasing services, under any plausible market definition.
