4.2.1.2. The Notifying Parties’ views

(38)    The Notifying Parties consider that the market should be defined as a separate
        market for the wholesale supply of leased lines, including dark fibre, noting that
        active and passive leased lines are substitutes from a demand- and supply-side
        perspective.43

(39)    From a demand-side perspective, the Notifying Parties argue that the decision
        whether to purchase a dark fibre line or an active leased line depends on the
        customer’s preference as to what to do with the rented lines, i.e., install and use its
        own equipment enabling full control over the technical parameters (passive access)
        or obtain a lesser degree of control over these parameters by continuing to use the

4.4.      Wholesale internet connectivity services

(66)      As the Internet works as a network of networks, a local Internet Service Provider
          (“ISP”) who wants to offer internet services to end-customers must connect with
          other networks in order to allow its end-customers to exchange traffic with other
          end-customers / content providers beyond its own local network. In order to reach
          networks at far distance, an ISP has to connect to larger networks, which can link
          two ISPs to each other. Such connectivity can be acquired either by peering (the
          mutual and free exchange of traffic between two networks) or by transit (the
          provision, for a fee, of access to the Internet via a network). Peering offers access
          only to the customers of the other network whereas a transit supplier gives access
          to the whole Internet. Peering usually occurs between ISPs of like size and
          geographical reach. Smaller networks can usually obtain connectivity to the larger
          ones only by paying for transit.73

(67)      The wholesale supply of internet connectivity services is situated upstream from
          the retail supply of internet services and business connectivity services because
          internet connections are a necessary input for the provision of such retail level
          internet services to customers.

          contributed to NetCo. Fluvius is not active on this market.

5.3.      Analytical framework

(190)     Article 2 of the Merger Regulation requires the Commission to examine whether
          notified concentrations are compatible with the internal market, by assessing
          whether they would significantly impede effective competition in the internal
          market or in a substantial part of it, in particular through the creation or
          strengthening of a dominant position.195

(191)     In this respect, a merger may entail horizontal and/or non-horizontal (i.e. vertical or
          conglomerate) effects. Horizontal effects are those deriving from a concentration
          where the undertakings concerned are actual or potential competitors of each other
          in one or more of the relevant markets concerned. Non-horizontal effects are those
          deriving from a concentration where the undertakings concerned are active in
          different relevant markets.196

         characterised by significant growth, which has attracted and can be expected to

5.5.1.1. Input foreclosure

5.5.1.1.1.        The Notifying Parties’ views

(253)       The Notifying Parties submit that the Transaction will not give rise to vertical input
            foreclosure concerns in relation to NetCo’s activities on the market for wholesale
            broadband access by restricting access to NetCo’s network for competing providers
            of retail telecommunications services as it does not create or enhance any vertical
            relationship between the wholesale supply of broadband access and relevant
            downstream markets.291

(254)       First, the Transaction facilitates the roll-out and growth of a future FTTP network.
            NetCo will offer an alternative to any operator for realising high bandwidth access
            to customers. The Notifying Parties have committed that NetCo will offer access to
            the network on an open, transparent and non-discriminatory basis to any operator
            who requests access.

(255)       Second, Fluvius only has very limited activities on the wholesale broadband market
            (increment of less than [0-5]%), so the market environment will not change.

(256)       Third, the Transaction will not result in an increase of any hypothetical pre-existing
            ability or incentive to engage in input foreclosure.

(257)       Pre-Transaction, Telenet is a fully vertically integrated supplier at wholesale and
            retail level. Any hypothetical ability or incentive to foreclose competitors would
            therefore already exist pre-Transaction and would not be merger specific. The only
            merger-specific effect is to reduce any such pre-existing ability or incentive on the
            part of Telenet to pursue a foreclosure strategy, since the Transaction results in a
            dilution of Telenet’s control in the upstream market by introducing Fluvius’s joint
            control through the NetCo. Moreover, NetCo has committed to operate as an open
            network granting non-discriminatory access to its fibre network.

(258)    Fluvius’s incentives in the context of NetCo are to ensure maximised profits for
         NetCo in the upstream market because Fluvius will not retain any downstream
         retail operations post-Transaction. A profit-maximising strategy from Fluvius’s
         perspective necessarily requires granting open access to retail operators competing
         with Telenet, because Fluvius would have nothing to gain from foreclosure. NetCo
         also does not have the incentive to engage in input foreclosure but has the explicit
         task and incentive to provide non-discriminatory and open access to broadband
         wholesale customers, [...] and as was also consistently indicated in all public press
         releases issued by Fluvius and Telenet, thereby allowing for free competition
         between telecommunications companies. [...]. Without ample demand from other
         operators, [...]. Telenet is therefore incentivised to maximise NetCo’s wholesale

(259)    Fourth, the structural separation of the upstream activities of Telenet from its

         separation, Chinese walls will be implemented between Telenet’s wholesale
         department (i.e., post-Transaction NetCo’s wholesale department) and Telenet

(260)    For the reasons set out below, while the upstream market shares are significant in

5.5.1.2.2.3.     Impact on effective competition

(283)    The Commission considers that due to the lack of ability and incentive, it is not
         needed to assess whether any customers foreclosure strategy would have a negative
         impact on effective competition.

(284)    In any event, as indicated in paragraph (273), most respondents to the market
         investigation consider that this Transaction will not have a negative impact on their
         business or on the relevant markets.

5.5.2.1.1.      The Notifying Parties’ views

5.5.2.1.1.1.    Ability to engage in input foreclosure

(291)    The Notifying Parties consider that there will be no input foreclosure whereby they
         would restrict wholesale access to NetCo’s dark fibre network for competing
         providers of wholesale and/or retail telecommunications services.300
(292)    First, the combined share of Telenet and Fluvius on the market for the wholesale
         supply of dark fibre (segment of the separate market for wholesale supply of leased
         lines) is moderate at only [10-20]% in Belgium, [20-30]% in Flanders and
         [20-30]% in Telenet footprint/coverage area by revenue as well as [10-20]%,
         [10-20]% and [10-20]% respectively by volume. This low to moderate market
         share is not indicative of the “significant degree of market power in the upstream
         market” that is required for input foreclosure to be a concern. Indeed, neither Party
         is an essential provider of dark fibre. Telenet itself relies on wholesale access to
         various third party leased lines in order to carry out its own retail operations, which
         creates inter-dependency.
(293)    Second, downstream suppliers will continue to have sufficient alternatives to turn
         to. Eurofiber will continue to be the clear market leader for the supply of dark fibre,
         with [50-60]% of the market in Belgium and Flanders and [50-60]% in Telenet
         footprint/coverage area by revenue as well as [50-60]%, [50-60]% and [50-60]%
         respectively by volume. Proximus will be number three in the market, and Elia is
         also a significant supplier of dark fibre. There are several other dark fibre suppliers
         who offer credible alternatives including Sofico, Verixi, Colt and Fluxys.301

(294)    The Parties’ competitors therefore offer credible alternatives to that of Telenet
         ServCo post-Transaction.

(295)    Third, active leased lines are an alternative to dark fibre. When the constraint
         imposed by active leased lines are taken into account, there are even more
         alternative supplies for downstream rivals. Telenet ServCo’s market share on the
         separate leased lines market is only [10-20]% in Belgium, [10-20]% in Flanders
         and [10-20]% in Telenet footprint/coverage area by revenue, whereas Proximus (by
         far the market leader) has a market share of [40-50]% in Belgium, [40-50]% in
         Flanders and [40-50]% in Telenet footprint/coverage area.302

(296)    In fact, high-quality access to Proximus’s copper and fibre networks is guaranteed
         by regulation. In 2019, the BIPT imposed a regulatory obligation on Proximus to
         grant (i) active wholesale access to third parties to its copper pair network as well
         as its fibre network, and (ii) passive wholesale access to third parties to its fibre
         network. Proximus is obliged to provide a non-discriminatory non-exclusive open
         access for all interested third parties.303 Consequently, downstream competitors can
         also rely on a regulated alternative on the leased lines market, in addition to
         seeking commercial access.

(299)    In addition to not having any ability to foreclose inputs, the Notifying Parties

(300)    First, Telenet’s wholesale business is acting independently of its retail business
         based on a comprehensive ‘Chinese wall’ policy. Telenet’s wholesale department

         operations. The decisions at Telenet’s wholesale level will therefore be taken

         creates inter-dependency. No supplier of leased lines (wholesale and retail, active
         and passive) can provide its services 100% on its own network due to the black
         spots of their network coverage. Moreover, it is often commercially more
         advantageous to source from third parties than to extend dark fibre networks on a
         greenfield basis. Accordingly, there is extensive cross-supply, such that suppliers
         are interdependent on supplies from each other.
