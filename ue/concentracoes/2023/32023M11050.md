4.1.1.2. The Commission’s past practice

(10)      Flavours are compounds of naturally occurring and/or synthetically manufactured
          ingredients to impart flavour to materials designated for human consumption or
          animal feeding.11 With regard to the product market definition, the Commission has
          previously considered the existence of a single market for flavours. Flavours are
          almost always customised, i.e. designed for a specific end-product. However, due
          to the very high degree of supply-side substitutability, it was held that flavours
          constitute a single market.12 The Commission has also considered whether flavours
          should be further segmented by application (e.g. flavours for savoury food, sweet
          food, dairy, beverages and pharmaceuticals) and between "natural" and
          "non-natural" flavours, but the market definition was ultimately left open.13 With
          regard to the geographic dimension, the Commission previously considered that the
          market for flavours is at least EEA-wide and potentially global.14

4.2.1.2. The Commission’s assessment

(46)      As follows from the Guidelines on the assessment of non-horizontal mergers under
          the Council Regulation on the control of concentrations between undertakings
          (‘Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines’),53 a vertical concentration involves
          companies that are active on different relevant markets, operating at different levels
          of the supply chain. Generally, the vertical relationship is one where the
          ‘downstream’ firm purchases the output from the ‘upstream’ firm and uses it as an
          input in its own production, which it then sells on to its customers.

(47)      In light of the above, the Transaction will in the following be assessed in relation to
          the (downstream) markets in which PAI Partners’ portfolio companies are active,
          and more specifically, the markets where the Target’s food ingredients are used as
          input, i.e. savoury food products. Among all of the savoury food products
          manufactured and sold by PAI Partners’ different portfolio companies, there are
          vertically affected markets only with regard to LFF’s and Ecotone’s production and
          supply of (i) smoked herring, (ii) vegetable-based bread toppings and
          (iii) plant-based milk, each in France.

         competitors can offer food ingredients, and since the size of Ecotone’s and LFF’s
         purchases of the relevant food ingredients are […]. Ecotone and LFF would
         therefore not constitute a sufficient customer base that could be the basis for a
         customer foreclosure strategy. Even if Ecotone were to purchase all of its demand
         for cultures and functional ingredients from the Target, and LFF were to purchase
         all its demand for flavours from the Target, rival suppliers would not be foreclosed
         from supplying these ingredients to other downstream manufacturers of smoked
         herring, vegetable-based bread-toppings and plant-based milk. A customer
         foreclosure strategy is therefore not likely.

6.       CONCLUSION

         notified operation and to declare it compatible with the internal market and with the
         EEA Agreement. This decision is adopted in application of Article 6(1)(b) of the
         Merger Regulation and Article 57 of the EEA Agreement.
