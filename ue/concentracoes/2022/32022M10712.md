4.1.    Introduction

(7)     Among other portfolio companies, Apollo controls MAFTEC Co. Ltd.
        (“MAFTEC”, Japan). MAFTEC produces and supplies polycrystalline fibre wool

(8)     PCW is a fibre typically made from alumina and silica, available in a range of

(9)     MAFTEC is a vertically integrated manufacturer of PCW. Its business is divided in

(10)    Tenneco purchases PCW support mats and uses them as an input for the

        aftertreatment systems and respective hot-ends are typically sold to automotive
        OEMs. Finally, Tenneco uses small quantities of PCW support mats to

4.2.      Manufacture and supply of PCW blankets

4.2.1.    Product market definition

(19)      The Commission has not previously defined a market for the manufacture and
          supply of PCW blankets.6

4.2.1.1. The Notifying Party’s views

(20)      The Notifying Party submits that PCW blankets can be manufactured using
          different processes. First, PCW blankets can be distinguished between needle-
          punched and non-needle-punched PCW blankets. Needle-punched blankets
          interlock the fibres together in a flat two-dimensional shape. These blankets are
          cheaper to produce, and have a greater tensile and peeling strength than non-
          needle-punched PCW blankets.7 Second, PCW blankets can be distinguished
          between dry-laid and wet-laid PCW blankets. Wet-laid PCW blankets have a
          higher manufacturing cost than dry-laid ones and have slightly lower heat
          resistance. However, wet-laid PCW is more suitable and less expensive, when a
          thicker supporting mat is required.8

(21)      The Notifying Party further notes that MAFTEC only manufactures needle-
          punched, dry-laid PCW blankets.9

          Conclusion

(39)      In light of the market investigation, the Commission therefore finds that:

(40)      First, PCW blankets are not interchangeable with other forms of PCW, such as
          bulk or mats.

(41)      Second, based on its product characteristics, blankets made from PCW are
          interchangeable only to a limited extent with blankets made from alternative
          materials, at least in the EEA. PCW seems to be the preferred material for
          manufacturers of hot-ends due to its higher heat resistance compared to e.g. AES
          and RCF. Manufacturers of hot-ends for gasoline powered light vehicles therefore
          typically do not consider alternative insulation materials as being substitutable for
          PCW. However, AES is being used by hot-end manufacturers in hot-ends for diesel
          cars and CTOHs. Moreover, outside the EEA, RCF is being used by hot-end
          manufacturers in hot-ends for gasoline and diesel cars, and CTOHs. It follows that
          PCW could face competition from alternative materials at least to some degree.

(42)      Third, with regard to a potential distinction between dry-laid and wet-laid PCW,
          the Commission notes that dry-laid PCW is the preferred material for the
          manufacture of hot-ends for gasoline vehicles, due to its higher heat resistance in
          thinner layers, compared to wet-laid PCW. Wet-laid PCW, however, is suitable for
          use in the manufacture of hot-ends for diesel cars and CTOHs. Wet-laid PCW is
          also suitable for gasoline vehicles, however this would require the use of thicker
          layers of the material, compared to dry-laid PCW.36

(43)      Fourth, there is no need to further distinguish between needle-punched and non-
          needle-punched PCW blankets.

(44)      The Commission considers that, for the purposes of the present case, it may be left
          open whether PCW belongs to the same product market with other types of
          insulating materials, and whether, within the plausible market for PCW blankets,
          there should be a further distinction between dry-laid and wet-laid PCW, as the
          Transaction does not lead to serious doubts as to its compatibility with the internal
          market, regardless of the market definition adopted. Since MAFTEC only
          manufactures dry-laid PCW blankets, the Commission will assess the Transaction
          under the most precautionary basis, considering a market for dry-laid PCW
          blankets.

4.3.1.2. The Commission’s assessment

(52)    The market investigation confirmed that the manufacture of PCW support mats
        constitutes a market that is separate from the market for the manufacture of PCW

(53)    First, the market investigation confirmed that processing PCW blankets into mats

(54)    Second, the market investigation confirmed that, from the demand-side of the

        manufacturers of hot-ends were unanimous in that they would source PCW only in
        the form of mats, and would not source PCW blankets instead.48 This was also
        confirmed by the responses of manufacturers of PCW blankets and mats.49

(55)    Third, hot-end manufacturers cannot easily switch to mats made from different
        materials or by different procedures during the course of an ongoing OEM
        program, other than the one that was validated by the OEM as part of the overall
        validation of the hot-end. Each different type of mat has different specifications,
        which are taken into account in the initial design of the hot-end. The initial design
        for the hot-end is typically validated by the OEM and any subsequent change to its

5.2.    Analytical framework

(88)    The legal test for the assessment of vertical effects of a merger is set out in the
        Merger Regulation and the Commission’s Guidelines on the assessment of non-
        horizontal mergers under the Council Regulation on the control of concentrations
        between undertakings (“Non-horizontal Merger Guidelines”).70

(89)    According to the Non-horizontal Merger Guidelines, foreclosure effects in a
        vertical scenario may occur where actual or potential rivals’ access to supplies (i.e.
        input foreclosure) or markets (i.e. customer foreclosure) is hampered or eliminated
        as a result of the merger, thereby reducing these companies’ ability and/or
        incentive to compete.71

(90)    In assessing the likelihood of an anticompetitive input foreclosure scenario, the
        Commission examines, whether the merged entity would have, post-merger, the
        ability to substantially foreclose access to inputs, whether it would have the
        incentive to do so, and whether a foreclosure strategy would have a significant
        detrimental effect on competition downstream.72 Those conditions are
        cumulative.73

        scenario, the Commission examines whether the merged entity would have the
        ability to foreclose access to downstream markets by reducing its purchases from
        its upstream rivals, whether it would have the incentive to reduce its purchases
        upstream, and whether a foreclosure strategy would have a significant detrimental

5.4.      Partial input foreclosure – Raising downstream rivals’ costs

(128)     Instead of withholding PCW blanket supplies from its customers in the
          manufacture and supply of PCW support mats, MAFTEC could increase the prices
          for supplies to such customers, while itself supplying PCW mats to Tenneco at
          lower than market prices. Such increase in prices of PCW blanket supplies would
          in theory be passed on to the manufacturers of hot-ends, who would in turn not be
          able to compete effectively against Tenneco.

5.4.1.    The Notifying Party’s views

(129)     As described in paragraph (97), the Notifying Party submits that there is no risk of
          input foreclosure as a result of the Transaction. This is mainly because (i) […],
          (ii) Tenneco cannot solely depend on MAFTEC for its sourcing of dry-laid PCW
          mats, and (iii) Tenneco would be subject to retaliation from OEMs, in case it
          engaged into a foreclosure theory, which would lead to an increase in prices of hot-
          ends. These arguments apply also in the context of a partial input foreclosure
          theory.

(130)     Furthermore, the Notifying Party submits that such partial input foreclosure would
          not lead to a substantial increase in the prices of Tenneco’s rival’s hot-ends, due to
          the trivial value of dry-laid PCW blankets compared to the final price of the hot-
          end.92

(131)     Moreover, the Notifying Party argues that an increase in the price of MAFTEC’s
          dry-laid PCW blankets would affect not only Tenneco’s rivals in the manufacture
          of hot-ends for gasoline vehicles in the EEA, where alternative materials are
          limited, but also manufacturers of hot-ends for diesel light-vehicles and CTOHs,
          for which blankets made from alternative materials constitute a viable substitute.
          Manufacturers of hot-ends for diesel light-vehicles and CTOHs could therefore
          switch to alternative sources (AES blankets, RCF blankets, etc.), which are not
          supplied by MAFTEC. In addition, Tenneco would also be impacted for the part of
          dry-laid PCW mats it does not source directly from MAFTEC.93
