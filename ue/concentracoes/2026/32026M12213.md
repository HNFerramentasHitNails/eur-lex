3.      INDUSTRY OVERVIEW
(11)    From a video game’s12 conception until the moment it is played by consumers, two
        main market activities can be distinguished: (i) game developing and publishing;
        and (ii) game distribution and marketing.13

           a. Games are developed for PCs, game consoles, and mobile devices (such as
              smartphones and tablets). Game software development comprises the design,
              art, programming and testing of games, usually by one or more development
              studios. Game publishing involves making video games available to the public
              under various monetization models, including pay-to-play, free-to-play, or
              combinations of the two.

           b. Game distribution involves the distribution of games to the public in either
              physical or digital form, through (i) physical retail (online and “brick-and-
              mortar” shops) and (ii) online download or streaming, via digital storefronts,
              app stores, and/or subscription services. In the case of physical distribution,
              games are distributed on physical media like cartridges or compact discs and
              sold online or in brick-and-mortar stores. In the case of digital distribution,
              games are distributed through online download and/or streaming.

(12)    Linked to development and publishing of video games is also the organisation and
        commercialisation of Esports events, that can take place in person or online. Almost
        all events are live streamed or broadcast to reach a large and often global audience.14

(13)    Esports tournaments are organised by video game publishers or owners of video
        game IP (first-party organisers) or third-party Esports event organisers (third-party
        organisers). They involve organising sponsorships, broadcasting, streaming and
        media rights, managing prize pools, securing venues, attracting players and selling
        tickets. For video game publishers, the principal aim of organising Esports
        competitions is to engage their most committed players and create widely distributed
        content, to drive the overall popularity (and commercial success) of the relevant
        games. In short, they are used as a form of marketing. In contrast, for third-party
        Esports event organisers, the principal aim is revenue maximization from the event
        itself (including from the distribution of content related to the event), mainly from
        sponsorship and broadcast fees, media distribution, service fees, ticket sales,
        merchandise, advertising and licensing fees.15

4.2.1.     Relevant product market

4.2.1.1. Commission precedents

(43)      In Microsoft/ZeniMax39, the Commission considered whether the market for video
          game distribution should be segmented according to physical vs. digital distribution.
          Further, in relation to digital distribution, the Commission considered whether
          segmentations were appropriate by: (i) gaming platforms (PC, console and mobile);
          (ii) payment model (upfront payment vs. subscription); and (iii) type of access to
          games (download vs. streaming). The Commission left open the product market
          definition as regards physical vs. digital distribution and a possible segmentation by
          gaming platforms, whereas it found that a segmentation by payment model and type
          of access was not warranted.

(44)      In Microsoft/Activision Blizzard, the Commission concluded that the distribution of
          mobile video games does not belong to the same market as the distribution of PC
          and console video games, respectively.40

(45)      Further, the Commission concluded that the market for the distribution of PC and
          console video games should not be segmented between physical and digital
          distribution,41 nor based on payment model42 or type of access (download vs.
          streaming).43

(46)      The Notifying Party submits that the distribution of PC and console video games is

5.2.   Introduction

(75)    Under Article 2(2) and (3) of the Merger Regulation, the Commission must assess
        whether a proposed concentration would significantly impede effective competition
        in the internal market or in a substantial part of it, in particular through the creation
        or strengthening of a dominant position.

(76)    In this respect, a merger may entail horizontal and/or non-horizontal (namely,
        vertical or conglomerate) effects. Horizontal effects are those deriving from a
        concentration where the undertakings concerned are actual or potential competitors
        of each other in one or more of the relevant markets concerned. Vertical effects are
        those deriving from a concentration where the undertakings concerned are active on
        different or multiple levels of the supply chain. Conglomerate effects are those
        deriving from a concentration where the undertakings concerned are in a
        relationship which is neither horizontal nor vertical. A concentration may involve
        all three types of effects. In such a case, the Commission will appraise horizontal
        and non-horizontal effects in accordance with the guidance set out in the Horizontal
        Merger Guidelines71 and the Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines.72

(77)    In assessing the competitive effects of a merger, the Commission compares the
        competitive conditions that would result from the notified merger with the
        conditions that would have prevailed without the merger. In most cases, the
        competitive conditions existing at the time of the merger constitute the relevant
        comparison for evaluating the effects of a merger. However, in some circumstances,

5.4.    Vertical effects

5.4.1. Legal framework

(82)    According to the Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines, non-coordinated effects may
        significantly impede effective competition as a result of a vertical merger if such
        merger gives rise to foreclosure. Foreclosure occurs where actual or potential
        competitors' access to supplies or markets is hampered or eliminated as a result of
        the merger, thereby reducing those companies' ability and/or incentive to compete.83
        Such foreclosure may discourage entry or expansion of competitors or encourage
        their exit.84

(83)    The Non-Horizontal Guidelines distinguish between two forms of foreclosure. Input
        foreclosure occurs where the merger is likely to raise the costs of downstream
        competitors by restricting their access to an important input. Customer foreclosure
        occurs where the merger is likely to foreclose upstream competitors by restricting
        their access to a sufficient customer base.85

         services to video game publishers, it would have no impact because video game
         publishers have numerous alternatives to which they can turn to market their
         product, including numerous Esports alternatives. This would include using the
         services of a variety of other Esports organizers, organizing events themselves, or
         organizing the events by using the services of white label providers.101

         event organizers in the market. Moreover, game developers have the capability to
         organize events on their own, and there are many examples of successfully held
         events.103

5.4.3.2.1. The Notifying Party’s views

(109)       The Notifying Party submits that it would be economically irrational for it to
            foreclose or disadvantage rival game developers or publishers, for the following
            reasons.

(110)       First, Esports organisers would generate revenue by hosting events with the most
            popular titles to attract the greatest number of participants and viewers. EA’s titles
            would not be leading Esports titles, accounting for a […] proportion of events,
            viewership and prize pools across all Esports events globally.105

(111)       Second, EFG would make the vast majority of its revenues from sponsors, rather
            than game publishers. Such sponsors would seek to reach the largest possible
            audience for their products. None of EA’s games is a top 10 Esports game, with
            Apex Legends representing [0-5]% of total peak viewers in 2024, EA SPORTS FC
            representing [0-5]% of total peak viewers in 2024, and EA SPORTS Madden NFL
            a significantly smaller [0-5]%. If EFG therefore withheld Esports organizing from
            video game publisher rivals of EA, its Esport events would reach significantly fewer
            consumers, be correspondingly less attractive, and would therefore generate less
            revenue from sponsors. There would be no reason to suspect significant diversion
            from events featuring leading Esports titles like League of Legends, Counter-strike,

            through greater sales of EA games. Esports is not a critical input to video game
            publishing, and thus disadvantaging other game publishers in Esports would not
            result in greater sales of EA games.106
