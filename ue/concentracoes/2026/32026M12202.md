4.1.    Introduction to natural gas transport in Portugal
(6)     Producers sell and supply natural gas to wholesale suppliers, which, in turn,
        supply it to retail suppliers. The latter supply natural gas to final customers. The
        physical transport of the natural gas volumes between these different market
        operators is done via transportation networks (also referred to simply as ‘grids’).
(7)     The transportation of gas in Portugal involves two types of networks:
(a)     The transmission network, which is used by importers or producers to transport
        natural gas on behalf of wholesale suppliers that aim to resell it either to other gas
        wholesalers, to distributors, or to large industrial customers that are directly
        connected to the gas transmission network. The transmission network is
        composed of mostly high-pressure pipelines and is operated by the Transmission
        System Operator (‘TSO’), which in Portugal is REN Gasodutos (‘REN’), a part
        of the REN group (‘Redes Energéticas Nacionais’). (7)
(b)     The distribution networks, (8) which are used to deliver natural gas on behalf of
        retail suppliers to end customers. The distribution networks are composed of
        mostly medium pressure (between 20 and 4 bar) and low pressure (below 4 bar)
        pipelines and are operated by DSOs. In Portugal, there are three DSOs: Floene
        (jointly controlled by Marubeni), REN Portgás, and Sonorgás. Each distribution
        network covers a part of the Portuguese territory, not overlapping with each
        other. (9)

5.1.    Legal framework
(33)    According to the Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines, (38) foreclosure occurs
        when actual or potential rivals’ access to markets is hampered, thereby reducing
        those companies’ ability and/or incentive to compete. (39) Such foreclosure can
        take two forms: (i) input foreclosure, when access of downstream rivals to
        supplies is hampered (40) and (ii) customer foreclosure, when access of upstream
        rivals to a sufficient customer base is hampered. (41)
(34)    For input or customer foreclosure to be a concern, three conditions need to be met
        post-Transaction: (i) the merged entity needs to have the ability to foreclose its
        rivals; (ii) it needs to have the incentive to do so; and (iii) the foreclosure strategy
        needs to have a significant detrimental effect on competition on the downstream
        market (input foreclosure) or on consumers in the downstream market (customer
        foreclosure). (42) In practice, these factors are often examined together since they
        are closely intertwined.

5.2.2.    The Commission’s assessment
           Ability to foreclose
(42)      Input foreclosure may raise competition concerns if it concerns an important input
          for the downstream product and if the merged entity has a significant degree of
          power in the upstream market. (54) The Commission finds that these criteria are
          met with respect to Floene’s operation of its distribution network, as follows.
          First, Floene is a natural monopolist in the areas of its distribution networks (i.e.,
          it holds a 100% market share in each, as indicated in paragraph (15)). Second, the
          gas distribution services provided by Floene are a critical input in each network,
          as confirmed by the majority of respondents to the market investigation (55).
          Without access to Floene’s distribution networks, downstream retail suppliers are
          unable to supply natural gas to customers in the networks managed by Floene.
          Because Floene’s distribution services in each of its networks are a critical input
          for retail suppliers of natural gas Floene seems a priori to have an ability to
          foreclose.
(43)      However, the Commission finds that Floene is nevertheless unlikely to have the
          ability to engage in an input foreclosure strategy in any of the networks it
          manages, for the reasons set out below:
(44)      First, as confirmed by the majority of participants in the market investigation
          natural gas distribution activities in Portugal are subject to established sectoral
          regulation, and supervision and enforcement of the sectoral regulation is carried
          out effectively by ERSE (an independent administrative authority). The majority
          of respondents to the market investigation consider that, following the
          Transaction, Floene would not have the ability (i) to increase the tariffs charges
          for access to its distribution networks, (56) (ii) to deny access to its distribution
          networks to rivals of Factor Energia, (57) or (iii) to deteriorate the quality of access
          (through delays, decreasing capacity, or applying discriminatory terms to Factor
          Energia’s rivals). (58)

        Incentive to foreclose
(52)    When assessing the merged entity’s possible incentive to foreclose, the
        Commission assesses the degree to which foreclosure would be profitable. The
        vertically integrated firm will consider how its supplies of inputs to competitors
        downstream will affect not only the profits of its upstream activities, but also of
        its downstream activities. The merged entity faces a trade-off between the profit
        lost in the upstream market due to a reduction of input sales to (actual or potential)
        rivals and the profit gain, in the short or longer term, from expanding sales
        downstream or, as the case may be, being able to raise prices to consumers. (80)
(53)    The Commission finds it unlikely that in this case the merged entity would have
        the incentives to implement an input foreclosure strategy post-Transaction, for the
        following reasons.
(54)    First, the Commission finds that Floene’s networks are not capacity constrained
        and, therefore, it is not necessary to allocate scarce resources at the distribution
        level between different natural gas retailers. The absence of capacity constraints
        was confirmed by all respondents providing a substantive response in the market
        investigation (81) and by ERSE. (82) Further, the Portuguese regulatory regime
        includes detailed regulations regarding capacity and grid connections that DSOs
        must comply with and that are transparent to customers. (83) Therefore, the
        Commission finds that it is unlikely that Floene would have an incentive to prefer
        Factor Energia to another retail supplier of natural gas, since without capacity
        constraints and with precise deadlines to establish connections, any worsening of
        terms in allocating capacity to retail supplier would decrease Floene’s revenues
        in providing access to its networks.

5.2.3.    Conclusion on input foreclosure
(58)      For the reasons set out above, the Commission finds that it is unlikely that, post-
          Transaction, Floene would pursue an input foreclosure strategy to the benefit of
          Factor Energia’s natural gas retail business. This was also confirmed in the market
          investigation: all respondents that provided a substantive response confirmed that
          they did not expect the Transaction to have a significant impact on their
          company (85) or the retail supply of natural gas market. (86)
(59)      The Commission therefore concludes that the Transaction does not raise serious
          doubts as to its compatibility with the internal market or the functioning of the
          EEA Agreement in relation to potential foreclosure of Floene’s distribution
          networks to Factor Energia’s competitors on the downstream market of retail
