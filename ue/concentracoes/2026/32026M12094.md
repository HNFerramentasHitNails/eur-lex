4.1.      Product market definition

4.1.1.    Manufacture and supply of commercial refrigeration products

4.1.1.1. The Notifying Party’s arguments
(11)      As the Notifying Party explained, refrigerated cabinets may be categorised into
          two main system types: remote display cases, i.e., those relying on centralized
          refrigeration systems located elsewhere in the store or facility, (including direct
          expansion, water-loop (4), and indirect cascade systems) and plug-in display
          cases, i.e., self-contained portable units equipped with their own built-in cooling
          system that expels heat directly into the store environment, requiring only a power
          connection, also called ‘plug-ins’, (either water-cooled or air-cooled). (5) Within
          these two broad categories, differentiation is mainly driven by energy
          consumption, cooling performance, lifecycle cost, as well as by design
          adaptability to different store formats. (6)
(12)      The Notifying Party argued that there is one single product market for the supply
          of all commercial refrigeration equipment, comprised of both remote and plug-in
          refrigeration systems. (7) In particular, the Notifying Party claimed that both
          remote and plug-in display cases constitute alternative technical means to deliver
          the same commercial outcomes, i.e. temperature control, product preservation,
          and merchandising. (8)
(13)      Contrary to remote display cases, which rely on a centralised refrigeration system
          located elsewhere in the store or facility, plug-in display cases consist of self-
          contained portable units equipped with their own built-in cooling system that
          expels heat into the store environment, requiring only a power connection. (9) The
          Notifying Party explained that despite these technical differences, remote and
          plug-in display cases perform the same core function, which is storing and
          presenting perishable goods, such as dairy, meat, beverages, and packaged
          products in retail stores. (10) The Notifying Party added that in commercial
          practice, the technical distinctions among the above systems translate only into
          differences in suitability for specific store configurations, rather than into separate
          demand-side markets. (11) It also submitted that food retailers choose between
          remote, and plug-in display cases based on considerations such as available space,
          energy efficiency, maintenance preferences, and the required colling capacity. (12)

4.2.      Geographic market definition

4.2.1.    Manufacture and supply of commercial refrigeration products

4.2.1.1. The Notifying Party’s view
(32)      The Notifying Party considers that the market for the manufacture and supply of
          commercial refrigeration products, as well as its potential subsegments of remote
          display cases and plug-ins, are EEA-wide in scope. (45)
(33)      In particular, according to the Notifying Party, from the demand-side,
          multinational food retailers increasingly conduct centralized or regional
          procurement across the EEA. (46) Retail customers (not only large ones, but also
          medium- and small-sized food retailers) increasingly conduct tenders open to all
          EEA-based operators (and sometimes also to non-EEA players) for the supply of
          display cases. In this context, both domestic and international suppliers are
          selected based on appealing, yet cost-effective, commercial proposals,
          irrespective of their manufacturing facilities’ location or geographical
          coverage. (47) Large customers often negotiate prices at the EEA level in order to
          obtain better prices and they can easily compare and switch suppliers across
          borders. (48)
(34)      From the supply-side, the Notifying Party noted that leading manufacturers have
          sales and service networks covering the entire EEA. They usually manufacture
          display cases at a limited number of production sites and supply customers in
          multiple countries from these sites (absent any significant transportation
          costs). (49) Within the EEA, there are no national standards, regulatory or
          structural barriers that are preventing cross-border trade. (50) Moreover, non-EEA
          players are expected to gradually increase their market presence over time.
          According to the Notifying Party, this is particularly the case for Turkish
          manufacturers, which in recent years have already successfully participated in
          several tenders launched by European food retailers, thereby demonstrating their
          ability to compete effectively on both technical and commercial grounds. (51)
          Finally, the Notifying Party argued that transport costs [Epta’s cost structure]
          typically account for [Epta’s cost structure] between [5-10] – [10-20]% of the
          products’ selling price. (52)

5.2.      Legal Framework
(46)      Under Article 2(2) and (3) of the Merger Regulation, the Commission must assess
          whether a proposed concentration would significantly impede effective
          competition in the internal market or in a substantial part of it, in particular
          through the creation or strengthening of a dominant position. In this respect, a
          merger can entail horizontal and/or non-horizontal effects.
(47)      Horizontal effects are those deriving from a concentration where the undertakings
          concerned are actual or potential competitors of each other in one or more of the
          relevant markets concerned. The Commission appraises horizontal effects in
          accordance with the Horizontal Merger Guidelines. (68) According to
          paragraph 26 of the Horizontal Merger Guidelines, a non-exhaustive list of
          relevant factors needs to be assessed in order to determine whether significant
          non-coordinated effects are likely to result from a concentration.
(48)      Accordingly, the Commission will first assess market shares, closeness of
          competition between the Parties, important dynamics of the markets where the
          Parties’ activities overlap, the alternatives to the Parties, barriers to entry, as well
          as evidence of new entrants and the constraint exerted by competitors. Based on
          all these factors considered together, conclusions on horizontal non-coordinated
          effects are then drawn.

5.3.2.    The Parties’ combined market shares and market position are moderate
(52)      As shown in Section 5.1 above, the Parties have combined market shares in the
          supply of installation and after-sales maintenance and repair services in five
          Member States, which however do not exceed 30% in two of them, i.e. in Czechia
          and Germany, while they do not exceed 36% in the remaining three Member
          States (i.e., in Bulgaria, Cyprus and Romania), according to the market share
          estimates provided by the Notifying Party.
(53)      In all instances, the increments brought about by the Transaction do not exceed
          10%, while in one Member State, where the Parties achieve the highest combined
          market share, i.e. Bulgaria, the increment is lower than 3%.

          to the market investigation ascribe to the Parties a more important market position
