5.1.     Legal framework
(82)     Pursuant to Article 2(2) and (3) of the Merger Regulation, (123) the Commission
         must assess whether a concentration would significantly impede effective
         competition in the internal market or in a substantial part of it, in particular
         through the creation or strengthening of a dominant position. In this respect, a
         merger can entail horizontal and/or non-horizontal effects.
(83)     With particular regard to non-horizontal effects, a merger can entail such effects
         when it involves companies operating at different levels of the same value chain
         or in closely related markets.
(84)     In assessing potential vertical effects of a merger, the Commission analyses,
         among others, whether the merger results in foreclosure so that actual or potential
         rivals’ access to supplies or markets is hampered or eliminated as a result of the
         merger, thereby reducing those companies’ ability and/or incentive to
         compete. (124) Such foreclosure may discourage entry or expansion of rivals or
         encourage their exit. Foreclosure can be found even if the foreclosed rivals are
         not forced to exit the market. It is sufficient that the rivals are disadvantaged and
         consequently led to compete less effectively. Such foreclosure is regarded as anti-
         competitive where the merging companies — and, possibly, some of their
         competitors as well — are as a result able to profitably increase the price charged
         to consumers.
(85)     The Guidelines on the assessment of non-horizontal mergers under the Council
         Regulation on the control of concentrations between undertakings (‘NHMG’)
         distinguish between two forms of foreclosure: (i) input foreclosure, when access
         of downstream rivals to supplies is hampered; (125) and (ii) customer foreclosure,
         when access of upstream rivals to a sufficient customer base is hampered. (126)
(86)     In assessing both types of foreclosure, the Commission assesses whether the
         merged entity (i) would have the ability to engage in foreclosure, (ii) whether it
         would have the incentive to do so, and (iii) what would be the overall impact on
         effective competition in the affected markets.
(87)     The Commission will assess in the sections below whether the Transaction gives
         rise to serious doubts due to vertical non-coordinated effects on all vertically
         affected markets.

5.2.2.     Competitive assessment
(91)       In Swecon’s Exclusive Territories, the Transaction does not give rise to
           horizontally affected markets but only vertically affected markets between VCE’s
           activities upstream, on the market for the manufacture and supply of construction
           equipment, the supply of spare parts as well as diagnostic software solutions for
           the maintenance and repair of construction equipment; and Swecon’s activities’
           downstream on the markets for the distribution of construction equipment and the
           supply of after-sales services. In view of the results of the market investigation,
           however, these markets do not give rise to serious as to the compatibility of the
           Transaction with the internal market and the EEA agreement.

5.2.2.1. Input foreclosure
(92)       The results of the market investigation confirm that the Transaction will not
           foreclose Swecon’s competitors for the distribution of construction equipment or
           the supply of after-sales services. Indeed, VCE does not currently sell Volvo
           construction equipment, spare parts or diagnostic software solutions to
           independent dealers in these countries. (137) This is consistent with the fact that
           the market investigation did not elicit any input foreclosure concern from
           Swecon’s competitors in its Exclusive Territories. (138)
(93)       Given that these competitors do not currently have access to Volvo construction
           equipment, spare parts and TechTool: (i) the new entity will not be able to further
           restrict their access to these products, (ii) the Transaction will not change the

(94)      By the same token, the results of the market investigation confirm that the

5.3.2.2.1.3.    Impact
(138)    Overall, the results of the market investigation confirm that a number of elements
         would limit the impact that any restriction of Swecon’s passive sales to
         independent MROs in VCE’s Reserved Territories may have.
(139)    First, the market investigation confirmed that Volvo construction equipment
         account for only a limited share of all construction equipment sold in the EEA
         and, at national level, in VCE’s Reserved Territories. According to the Parties’
         estimates, Volvo’s market shares for the distribution of construction equipment
         (overall) (206) remain modest (i.e. below 30%) in Denmark ([20-30]% in 2024),
         Finland ([20-30]% in 2024), France ([10-20]% in 2024), Italy ([5-10]% in 2024)
         and Poland ([10-20]% in 2024). (207) The only exception is Norway where
         Volvo’s market share is higher ([40-50]%) but the Parties could not identify any
         independent MRO offering after-sales services for Volvo construction equipment
         in this country and the market investigation did not elicit any concern in this
         country in particular. The information collected in the course of the market
         investigation confirmed the reliability of these market share estimates. (208)
(140)    Second, Swecon’s passive sales of spare parts outside its territory, including into
         VCE’s Reserved Territories, are occasional and marginal and are estimated to
         represent [0-5]% in each of these countries. (209) This is consistent with the data
         collected from one of the complainants showing that Swecon accounts for only
         part of the spare parts it sources from third parties. (210)
(141)    Third, a significant number of end customers have their own in-house workshops
         and would thus be unaffected by any restriction of Swecon’s passive sales to
         independent MROs in VCE’s Reserved Territories. (211)

5.3.2.2.2.      Customer foreclosure
(144)    The market investigation did not elicit any concern with respect to a possible
         restriction in the access of VCE’s competitors upstream for the supply of spare
         parts to MROs active downstream in the provision of after-sales services for
         construction equipment. This is consistent with the fact that Swecon only
         purchases and sells Volvo-branded spare parts and does not purchase third-party
         spare parts. (213) It follows that the new entity will not have the ability to restrict
         the access of competing spare part manufacturers to MROs in VCE’s Reserved
         Territories and the Transaction will not change the incentive of the Parties in this
         respect.

5.3.2.2.3.      Conclusion
(145)    It follows from the above that the vertical links between the Parties’ activities for
         the supply of spare parts and/or diagnostic software solutions (upstream) and
         VCE’s activities for the provision of after-sales services for construction
         equipment in VCE’s Reserved Territories (downstream) do not give rise to serious
         doubts as to the compatibility of the Transaction with the internal market and the
         EEA Agreement.


6.       CONCLUSION
(146)    For the above reasons, the European Commission has decided not to oppose the
         notified operation and to declare it compatible with the internal market and with
         the EEA Agreement. This decision is adopted in application of Article 6(1)(b) of
         the Merger Regulation and Article 57 of the EEA Agreement.
