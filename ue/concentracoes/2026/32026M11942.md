4.1.2.    The Parties’ views
(17)      The Parties essentially agree with the distinction between mail and parcel delivery
          services and freight forwarding. However, the Parties note that DHL Parcel Iberia
          also manages the shipment and delivery of pallets by truck, with pallets exceeding
          31.5 kg. These pallet delivery services refer to groups of parcels with the same
          sender and recipient, palletized solely to facilitate their management and handling.
          In the Parties’ view, this activity cannot be assimilated to freight forwarding, as
          pallet deliveries are carried out within the company’s standard parcel delivery
          infrastructure, are shipped every day and are delivered with the same delivery
          times as standard parcels. (16)
(18)      The Parties also agree with a market segmentation between express parcel
          delivery services and standard/deferred parcel delivery services. The Parties
          consider express delivery to be based on time-definite deliveries (TDI), that
          guarantee delivery before a specific time on a specific day (usually next day). (17)
(19)      Furthermore, the Parties agree with the market segmentation based on domestic
          and international delivery. Within international delivery, the Parties also consider
          that there are significant differences between intra-EEA and extra-EEA deliveries
          due to customs clearance processes. (18)
(20)      The Parties agree with the Commission’s differentiation by customer type in B2B,
          B2C and C2X, while noting that CTT Expresso typically manages all deliveries

4.2.      Geographic market definition

4.2.1.    The Commission’s previous practice
(27)      In the past, the Commission has considered that the geographic scope of the
          markets for the provision of parcel delivery services, and its subsegments, are
          likely national in scope. In particular, contracts for parcel deliveries are mostly
          negotiated at national level for both domestic and international deliveries, and
          parcel delivery companies need a significant national presence in a given country
          (e.g. infrastructure, client contracts, sales force) in order to meaningfully compete
          in that country. The Commission also noted that there may be a tendency towards
          broader markets. (31)

4.2.2.    The Parties’ views
(28)      The Parties submit that due to their similar characteristics and geographic
          proximity, there are arguments to consider Portugal and Spain as a single
          domestic Iberian market, as both Parties operate a similar network for their parcel
          delivery services. (32) In their view, the Iberian market can be seen from both the
          supply and demand side as one region. (33)
(29)      The Parties further submit that all the main operators present in Portugal or Spain

(30)      With respect to out-of-home delivery services, as regards geographic scope, the

        services can be left open, since the Transaction does not raise serious doubts as to

        plausible product sub-segments on the Spanish and Portuguese markets, as the
        narrowest plausible geographic markets.

5.1.    Horizontal effects
(35)    Under Article 2(2) and (3) of the Merger Regulation, the Commission must assess
        whether a proposed concentration would significantly impede effective
        competition in the internal market or in a substantial part of it, in particular as a
        result of the creation or strengthening of a dominant position. When assessing
        concentrations between actual or potential competitors, the Commission
        investigates whether such concentrations result in adverse “non-coordinated

          effects” and/or in “coordinated effects”, as described in the Guidelines on the
          assessment of horizontal mergers under the Merger Regulation (the “Horizontal
          Merger Guidelines”). (39)
(36)      Non-coordinated effects occur due to the removal of an important competitive
          constraint to the parties to a concentration, typically the loss of competition
          between the firms in the concentration. To assess the competitive constraints
          faced by the merging parties, the Horizontal Merger Guidelines include certain
          factors that the Commission uses to measure the likelihood and magnitude of non-
          coordinated effects. Such factors include: (i) the market shares of the parties; (ii)
          whether the merging firms are close competitors; (iii) whether there are limited
          possibilities for customers to switch to the parties’ competitors; (iv) whether the
          parties’ competitors could increase supply if prices increase; (v) whether the
          merged entity would be able to hinder its competitors’ expansion; and (vi)
          whether the merger removes a competitor that is stronger than its market shares
          suggest. By considering these and other factors, the Commission assesses whether
          a potential increase in market power is likely to lead to a significant impediment

5.1.2.1.8.       Conclusion
(93)     Overall, the Commission does not consider that serious doubts arise as to the
         compatibility of the Transaction with the internal market with regards to the
         market for parcel delivery services in Portugal or any plausible narrower markets.
         While each Party has an appreciable position in certain plausible markets, these
         strengths are mainly complementary. The market share increment resulting from
         the Transaction is either negligible or rather moderate, and/or the combined
         market shares remain moderate. Moreover, the market structure in the plausible
         relevant markets will remain similar post-Transaction. In particular, the merged
         entity will continue to face competitive pressure from numerous competitors,
         including Correos, DPD, IDS, MRW, NACEX, the integrators (UPS and FedEx)
         and new entrants (Paack and InPost). Finally, at least large customers are likely
         to exert a certain degree of bargaining and buyer power towards parcel delivery
         providers.
