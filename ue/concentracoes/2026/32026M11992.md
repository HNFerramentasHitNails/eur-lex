5.        RELEVANT MARKETS
(17)      The wholesale market of global telecommunication services (“GTS”) involves
          the provision of transmission capacity i.e., the data throughput available on a
          telecommunications link, expressed in terms of bandwidth (Terabits per second,
          Tbps), and comprising both the total installed physical capacity of the
          infrastructure (wet capacity) and the portion thereof that has been activated for
          commercial or operational use (lit capacity) on telecommunications infrastructure
          (typically international cable networks, such as submarine cables) to other
          telecommunications companies and business communications providers.
(18)      For the assessment of the Transaction, the following business activities of the
          Parties are relevant: (i) wholesale capacity on submarine cables; (ii) the provision
          of cable landing stations (“CLS”); and (iii) wholesale of internet connectivity
          services, specifically in what concerns backhaul connectivity between CLS and
          data centres (“CLS backhaul connectivity services”).
(19)      Considering the Complaint, the Parties’ activities as well as other concerns
          emerged in the market investigation, for the purposes of this Decision, the
          Commission will focus its relevant markets’ assessment on whether the following
          services could constitute separate markets (from the broader GTS market and
          between themselves): (i) wholesale capacity on submarine cables (Section 5.1);
          (ii) provision of CLS (Section 5.2); and (iii) CLS backhaul connectivity services
          (Section 5.3).

5.2.      Cable Landing Stations
(39)      For background, a CLS is a specialized, secure facility where a submarine cable
          physically lands, receives power, and interconnects with terrestrial networks. The
          related infrastructure typically includes the beach landing facilities and the
          terrestrial connections linking the CLS to backhaul networks or data centres.

5.2.1.    Product market definition

5.2.1.1. Commission’s precedents
(40)      The Commission has not previously assessed the provision of CLS as a potential
          separate market.

5.2.1.2. Notifying Parties’ views
(41)      The Notifying Parties submit that there is no plausible market for the provision of
          CLS. (20) A CLS is a technical facility, essentially a “big plug” for submarine
          cables. The landing party i.e., the entity operating the CLS, is responsible for the
          wet-to-dry transition of the cable, including securing beach landing permits,
          constructing the beach manhole, supervising the marine operations of pulling the
          cable ashore and ensuring proper protection of the shore section of the cable. The
          landing process is typically performed by a telecom operator. (21)
(42)      While CLS would be an integral part of the submarine cable system, as a
          component of the infrastructure used to provide wholesale GTS, the Notifying
          Parties argue that access to CLS is not provided on the downstream market
          separately from the capacity on the submarine cable. (22) Therefore, the market-
          facing services provided at CLS would be:
          (a)     Wholesale capacity on submarine cables, sold by cable operators or
                  consortia i.e., the purchase of transmission capacity on a cable system as
                  part of the wholesale market of GTS, which operators then use to provide
                  retail international connectivity to end customers; and
          (b)     Colocation/interconnection services, enabling customers, such as
                  hyperscalers, to access international connectivity. According to the
                  Notifying Parties, these services are generally not provided at the CLS
                  itself but at data centres, which are connected to the CLS via a backhaul
                  connectivity. The physical connection between the CLS and the data
                  centre can be operated by the cable consortium owning the cable or by a
                  qualified electronic communications operator (which may have its own
                  terrestrial network or just be a reseller). (23)

6.3.4.1. Plausible scope of any foreclosure theory of harm resulting from the Transaction
(134)    The Commission notes that the theory of harm, especially as articulated in the
         Complaint, is subject to a number of caveats.
(135)    First, the Commission notes that any plausible foreclosure theory of harm would
         affect providers of CLS backhaul connectivity services between an open CLS and
         a data centre. Indeed, the Commission notes that a closed CLS is operated and
         maintained typically by a member of a submarine cable consortium and designed
         to only host the backhaul connectivity of the CLS operator. In those CLS, multiple
         backhaul connections are either structurally impossible to achieve, or otherwise
         not currently in existence. As such, no third-party provider of CLS backhaul
         connectivity services could be foreclosed.
(136)    Second, the Commission notes that all CLS currently operated by Retelit are
         closed, except for the recent CLS in Savona. (106) The Commission notes that, as
         explained by the Notifying Parties, access to the capacity to the cable landing at
         Retelit’s new CLS in Savona, including for CLS backhaul connectivity services,
         is granted only by […]. (107) As such, since access to Retelit’s CLS for backhaul
         connectivity services is structurally impossible to achieve, not currently in
         existence (for the closed CLS) or otherwise not managed by Retelit itself (for the
         CLS in Savona), the Commission considers that third party providers of CLS
         backhaul connectivity services from Retelit’s CLS cannot be foreclosed as a
         consequence of the Transaction.
(137)    Third, regarding a hypothetical foreclosure theory of harm on future CLS, the
         Commission notes that, should the merged entity in the future decide to only
         deploy closed CLS, those CLS would require new CLS backhaul connectivity
         services, whose deployment costs would only be borne by CLS backhaul
         connectivity services providers when a new CLS backhaul connection would be
         needed. In other words, if an upcoming CLS is closed, third party CLS backhaul
         connectivity services providers would not invest in a new CLS backhaul
         connection. Accordingly, no plausible foreclosure theory of harm can occur
         regarding the deployment of future CLS.
(138)    In light of the above, the only CLS currently operated by Sparkle, and which are
         open by design, are the CLS in Palermo (108) and the CLS in Genoa. (109) The rest
         of this Section therefore only focuses on these two CLS, in which a plausible
         foreclosure theory of harm can be articulated. (110)
